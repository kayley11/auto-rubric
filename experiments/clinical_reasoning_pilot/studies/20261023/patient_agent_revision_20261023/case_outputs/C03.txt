Young adult with persistent bloody diarrhoea, urgency, cramping, morning back stiffness, and prior erythema nodosum, unresponsive to antibiotics, raising concern for inflammatory bowel disease with extra-intestinal manifestations.

2. Diagnostic possibilities
(1) Ulcerative colitis (suspected, not confirmed): bloody diarrhoea with urgency and cramping is typical; erythema nodosum and sacroiliitis (morning back stiffness) are recognised extra-intestinal features; antibiotic failure argues against infection. 3. Prioritised investigations

Colonoscopy with biopsies: to confirm or exclude IBD and determine subtype.
Stool culture and C. difficile toxin assay: to exclude infectious aetiology before immunosuppression.
CRP, ESR, full blood count: baseline inflammatory and anaemia assessment.
HLA-B27 and sacroiliac joint imaging (X-ray or MRI): to evaluate suspected sacroiliitis.

5. Safety and next steps
Exclude active infection before considering immunomodulatory therapy. Refer to gastroenterology for urgent colonoscopy. If sacroiliitis is confirmed, involve rheumatology. Monitor for complications such as significant rectal bleeding or dehydration.
