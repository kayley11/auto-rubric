Laboratory investigations revealed a normal full blood count and electrolyte panel, but elevated serum creatinine (132 μmol/L) and low serum albumin (31 g/L). Serum protein electrophoresis showed no monoclonal band, and serum free light chains were within normal limits. Given the combination of chronic refractory diarrhoea, autonomic features suggested by severe orthostatic hypotension and bradycardia, unexplained weight loss, and a raised NT-proBNP, systemic amyloidosis was suspected. Serum amyloid P component scintigraphy was not immediately available, so further evaluation proceeded with imaging and tissue biopsy. Echocardiography demonstrated concentric left ventricular wall thickening with a granular sparkling appearance, restrictive diastolic filling pattern, and a reduced ejection fraction of 42%, findings consistent with cardiac amyloid infiltration.
