Despite these measures, her symptoms persisted and gradually worsened, prompting readmission to the neurology department on 2 April 2026. On readmission, the patient reported persistent, severe, holocranial headache, photophobia, and new-onset nausea with two episodes of vomiting. She denied visual disturbances, limb weakness, sensory changes, seizures, or alterations in consciousness. General physical examination was unremarkable, and she was afebrile with stable vital signs. Neurological examination again revealed marked nuchal rigidity without focal deficits; fundoscopy was initially reported as normal. Given the refractory nature of her symptoms despite appropriate treatment for presumed viral lymphocytic meningitis, a broader diagnostic workup was initiated. Repeat CSF analysis demonstrated persistent lymphocytic pleocytosis (WBC 210 cells/μL; 95% lymphocytes), elevated protein (112.4 mg/dL), and normal glucose. Repeat PCR panel for common viral and bacterial pathogens, including herpes simplex virus, varicella-zoster virus, and enterovirus, was again negative. Autoimmune and paraneoplastic antibody panels in serum and CSF were unremarkable.
