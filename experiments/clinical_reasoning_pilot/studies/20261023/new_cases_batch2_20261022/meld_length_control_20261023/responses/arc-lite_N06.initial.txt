The aPTT elevation was attributed to the concurrent unfractionated heparin that had been running prior to the 17:00 pause, and the fibrinogen level was considered marginally adequate for ongoing thrombolysis. The interventional team elected to proceed with a second 20-mg alteplase dose at 18:15, having observed no new haemorrhagic complication beyond the already-managed arterial access-site oozing. A repeat coagulation panel at 18:40 showed platelets of 58 × 10⁹/L, fibrinogen of 1.12 g/L, and aPTT of 52.3 s. The falling fibrinogen was interpreted as consumption secondary to ongoing thrombolysis rather than a consumptive coagulopathy, given the absence of a rising D-dimer disproportionate to the expected lytic effect and the stable haemoglobin.

At 19:05, the patient developed new-onset haemoptysis, initially described as small-volume and self-limiting. The second alteplase infusion was halted at 19:10. A chest radiograph at 19:25 showed no new infiltrate or effusion. Haemoglobin remained stable at 9.4 g/dL. The multidisciplinary team convened at 19:40 to reassess the risk–benefit balance. Pulmonary angiography, performed at 20:10, demonstrated a significant reduction in the right main pulmonary artery filling defect compared with the pre-treatment study, with residual thrombus in the right lower lobe branches. The left pulmonary artery showed no residual obstruction.

Given the partial thrombolysis and the haemoptysis, the team decided against a third alteplase dose. The patient was transferred to the intensive care unit at 21:00 for close monitoring. Over the following 48 hours, serial coagulation studies showed a gradual return of fibrinogen to 2.1 g/L by 24 hours and 2.6 g/L by 48 hours, with platelet counts stabilising in the range of 55–70 × 10⁹/L without further transfusion. The haemoptysis resolved by 22:00 on the first post-procedure day. No further bleeding event was documented.

The patient remained on therapeutic anticoagulation with low-molecular-weight heparin from day 2, with a transition to oral rivaroxaban at discharge on day 9. Echocardiography at 72 hours showed resolution of right ventricular dilatation and normalisation of the tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion estimate. The final 24-hour alteplase exposure was 40 mg (0.47 mg/kg), administered in two 20-mg doses separated by 1 hour 55 minutes. The case was reviewed at the institutional morbidity and mortality conference, where the absence of a predefined platelet and fibrinogen threshold, the lack of a post-transfusion pre-infusion count, and the reliance on individual clinical judgment for dose selection were identified as areas for protocol development.
