However, given the persistent and worsening symptoms, a brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) with gadolinium contrast was obtained, which revealed a 2.3 × 1.8 cm ring-enhancing lesion in the left frontal lobe with surrounding vasogenic edema and mild mass effect on the adjacent lateral ventricle. No other lesions were identified. The patient was readmitted, and a repeat lumbar puncture was performed. CSF analysis now showed a WBC count of 210 cells/μL with 94% lymphocytes, protein of 98.6 mg/dL, and glucose of 38 mg/dL. CSF cytology revealed atypical large lymphoid cells with high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratios. Flow cytometry of the CSF demonstrated a monoclonal B-cell population expressing CD19, CD20, and CD22 with loss of CD5 and CD10. A stereotactic brain biopsy was performed under general anesthesia. Histopathological examination revealed a diffuse infiltration of atypical large lymphoid cells with centroblastic morphology. Immunohistochemistry confirmed positivity for CD20, PAX5, and MUM1, with a Ki-67 proliferation index of approximately 80%. The cells were negative for CD3, CD5, and CD10. The final diagnosis was primary central nervous system diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (PCNS-DLBCL).
