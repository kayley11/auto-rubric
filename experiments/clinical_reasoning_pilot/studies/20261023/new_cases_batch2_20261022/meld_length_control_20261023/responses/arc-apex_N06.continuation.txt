At 17:00 (t0), UFH was paused and the first 20-mg alteplase infusion began through right lower-limb deep venous access (Figures 1B). Arterial cannulation-site bleeding occurred at 17:20 and was managed with compression. The 17:39 sample showed platelets of 62 × 109/L, fibrinogen of 1.38 g/L, and aPTT of 47.5 s after the transfusions, and these values were reviewed before any decision on further thrombolysis. Because the patient remained hemodynamically unstable with ongoing catecholamine requirements and echocardiographic evidence of right ventricular dysfunction, a second 20-mg alteplase infusion was started at 18:10 and completed over 30 minutes. UFH remained suspended during both infusions and was resumed at 19:45 with a reduced initial bolus, given the recent fibrinolytic exposure and the arterial-line oozing. No intracranial hemorrhage occurred; surveillance imaging on the following day showed a small retroperitoneal hematoma that did not require intervention, and the arterial line site stabilized with sustained local pressure.

The clinical response over the subsequent hours was gradual rather than immediate. Norepinephrine requirements decreased progressively from 0.45 to 0.12 μg/kg/min by 06:00 the next morning, and lactate fell from 4.8 to 1.9 mmol/L over the same interval. Repeat echocardiography at 22:30 showed improvement in right ventricular size and systolic function, with a decrease in estimated pulmonary artery pressure and resolution of septal bowing. Follow-up CT angiography on hospital day 3 demonstrated marked reduction in the central pulmonary arterial thrombus burden, with residual peripheral filling defects. Platelet counts recovered spontaneously to 145 × 109/L by hospital day 4 without further transfusion, and HIT antibody testing by particle gel immunoassay returned positive with a strong optical density, later confirmed by serotonin release assay. Argatroban was not initiated because of the bleeding risk and the need for repeated procedures; instead, the patient was transitioned from UFH to fondaparinux on hospital day 5 once platelet counts exceeded 100 × 109/L and bleeding had fully subsided.

The patient was weaned from mechanical ventilation on hospital day 6 and discharged from the intensive care unit on hospital day 8. At three-month follow-up, she remained on apixaban without symptoms of dyspnea or exercise limitation, and echocardiography showed normal right ventricular function without evidence of chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension. This case illustrates that low-dose, sequentially administered alteplase, guided by bedside clinical assessment and concurrent correction of coagulopathy, may achieve hemodynamic stabilization in massive pulmonary embolism complicated by HIT-associated thrombocytopenia, while acknowledging that the dosing strategy described here was individualized and should not be interpreted as an established protocol. The decision-making process, including the rationale for dose fractionation, the thresholds used for blood product support, and the timing of anticoagulation resumption, is documented here to provide transparency for clinicians facing similar scenarios in which guideline recommendations are absent and the risks of both thrombosis and hemorrhage are high.
