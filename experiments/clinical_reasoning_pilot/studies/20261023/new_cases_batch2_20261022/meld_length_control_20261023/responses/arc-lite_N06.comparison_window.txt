The aPTT elevation was attributed to the concurrent unfractionated heparin that had been running prior to the 17:00 pause, and the fibrinogen level was considered marginally adequate for ongoing thrombolysis. The interventional team elected to proceed with a second 20-mg alteplase dose at 18:15, having observed no new haemorrhagic complication beyond the already-managed arterial access-site oozing. A repeat coagulation panel at 18:40 showed platelets of 58 × 109/L, fibrinogen of 1.12 g/L, and aPTT of 52.3 s. The falling fibrinogen was interpreted as consumption secondary to ongoing thrombolysis rather than a consumptive coagulopathy, given the absence of a rising D-dimer disproportionate to the expected lytic effect and the stable haemoglobin. At 19:05, the patient developed new-onset haemoptysis, initially described as small-volume and self-limiting. The second alteplase infusion was halted at 19:10. A chest radiograph at 19:25 showed no new infiltrate or effusion. Haemoglobin remained stable at 9.4 g/dL. The multidisciplinary team convened at 19:40 to reassess the risk–benefit balance. Pulmonary angiography, performed at 20:10, demonstrated a significant reduction in the right main pulmonary artery filling defect compared with the pre-treatment study, with residual thrombus in the right lower lobe branches.
