Laboratory investigations revealed a normal full blood count and electrolyte panel, but elevated serum creatinine (132 µmol/L) and low serum albumin (31 g/L). Serum protein electrophoresis showed no monoclonal band, and serum free light chains were within normal limits. Given the combination of chronic refractory diarrhoea, autonomic features suggested by severe orthostatic hypotension and bradycardia, unexplained weight loss, and a raised NT-proBNP, systemic amyloidosis was suspected. Serum amyloid P component scintigraphy was not immediately available, so further evaluation proceeded with imaging and tissue biopsy. Echocardiography demonstrated concentric left ventricular wall thickening with a granular sparkling appearance, restrictive diastolic filling pattern, and a reduced ejection fraction of 42%, findings consistent with cardiac amyloid infiltration. Cardiac magnetic resonance imaging showed diffuse subendocardial late gadolinium enhancement and elevated native T1 and extracellular volume values, supporting the diagnosis of cardiac amyloidosis. Technetium-99m pyrophosphate scintigraphy was performed and showed grade 2 myocardial uptake, but this was not diagnostic in isolation given the absence of a monoclonal protein. To establish the amyloid subtype, biopsy of the abdominal subcutaneous fat and subsequent rectal biopsy were undertaken. Congo red staining of the rectal specimen demonstrated apple-green birefringence under polarized light, confirming amyloid deposition. Mass spectrometry-based proteomic typing of the amyloid deposits identified transthyretin as the precursor protein, establishing a diagnosis of wild-type transthyretin amyloidosis with gastrointestinal and cardiac involvement. Genetic testing of the TTR gene revealed no pathogenic variant, consistent with the wild-type form. The patient's chronic diarrhoea was attributed to amyloid infiltration of the gastrointestinal tract causing autonomic neuropathy and malabsorption. He was commenced on tafamidis for disease-modifying therapy, along with supportive management including salt supplementation, compression stockings, and loperamide for symptom control. His diarrhoea frequency reduced to 1–2 episodes daily, and orthostatic symptoms improved modestly. At 6-month follow-up, NT-proBNP had stabilized and he had regained 3 kg in weight. This case highlights that persistent unexplained diarrhoea, particularly when accompanied by unexplained cardiac dysfunction and autonomic features, should prompt consideration of systemic amyloidosis, as delayed recognition remains common and early diagnosis permits initiation of disease-specific therapy.
