A 52-year-old man presented with a 1-year history of persistent watery diarrhoea (3–4 episodes daily), accompanied by acid reflux, upper abdominal discomfort, and numbness in the distal extremities. Previous gastroenterological investigations, including upper gastrointestinal endoscopy, had led to diagnoses of bile reflux gastritis and a colonic lesion treated by endoscopic submucosal dissection. Histopathological examination of the resected lesion showed a sessile serrated lesion with dysplasia. His diarrhoea remained refractory to symptomatic treatment. During the preceding 6 months, he also developed progressive exertional dyspnoea, fatigue, and unintentional weight loss of 10 kg. His medical history included gout and nephrolithiasis. There was no known family history of amyloidosis. On examination, his blood pressure was 80/46 mmHg and heart rate was 52 beats/min, with no other specific cardiopulmonary or abdominal abnormalities.
