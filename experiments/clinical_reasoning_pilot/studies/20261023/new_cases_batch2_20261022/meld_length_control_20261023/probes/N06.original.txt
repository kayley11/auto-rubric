The initial 20-mg alteplase dose was selected by individual clinical judgment because of the competing risks of persistent pulmonary arterial obstruction and bleeding. The intention was to assess the clinical response and bleeding before deciding whether to administer a further dose. This was not a guideline-defined regimen or a documented institutional protocol; expressing the total dose as 0.47 mg/kg describes exposure rather than the basis for dose selection.

Hematology recommended platelet transfusion according to bleeding and serial blood counts; the transfusion note documented cryoprecipitate for coagulation-factor replacement. Two institutional therapeutic doses of apheresis platelets were transfused from 14:50 to 15:10, with plasma, fibrinogen, and cryoprecipitate support before alteplase. No target platelet or fibrinogen threshold was documented, and no platelet count was obtained after transfusion but before the first infusion. The rise from 21 to 62 × 10⁹/L was interpreted primarily in relation to platelet transfusion, not as spontaneous recovery or an alteplase effect.

At 17:00 (t0), UFH was paused and the first 20-mg alteplase infusion began through right lower-limb deep venous access (Figures 1B). Arterial cannulation-site bleeding occurred at 17:20 and was managed with compression.

The 17:39 sample showed platelets of 62 × 10⁹/L, fibrinogen of 1.38 g/L, and aPTT of 47.5 s after hemostatic support. Nursing records documented a reduction in dopamine and norepinephrine rates to 8 and 2 mL/h at 18:10. The progress note documented effective compression and cessation of arterial cannulation-site bleeding at the 19:03 reassessment. Despite initial improvement, VA-ECMO and vasoactive support remained necessary. The team judged that further reperfusion might provide benefit after bleeding control and ordered another 20 mg at 19:03; infusion began at 19:25. No prespecified numerical escalation or stopping criteria were documented. Each stage lasted approximately 1 h; the total administered dose was 40 mg (0.47 mg/kg). Exact completion timestamps and volumetric alteplase rates were unavailable.

UFH was restarted at 6 mL/h after the second alteplase infusion, approximately 1 h after its 19:25 start, according to the treating team. The implied time of approximately 20:25 is not an independent execution timestamp. Preparation details and limitations are reported in Table 1. UFH was subsequently adjusted by aPTT through Day 10. Later orders included enoxaparin 4,000 anti-Xa IU every 12 h and fondaparinux 7.5 mg daily; discharge anticoagulation was edoxaban 60 mg daily. The patient-specific aPTT or anti-Xa target, exact transition times, and reasons for each switch could not be reconstructed. Additional plasma and cryoprecipitate were given; fibrinogen was 1.53 g/L at 00:41 on Day 1.
