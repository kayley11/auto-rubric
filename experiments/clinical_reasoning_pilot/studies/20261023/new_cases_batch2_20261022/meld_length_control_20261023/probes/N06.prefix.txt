The initial 20-mg alteplase dose was selected by individual clinical judgment because of the competing risks of persistent pulmonary arterial obstruction and bleeding. The intention was to assess the clinical response and bleeding before deciding whether to administer a further dose. This was not a guideline-defined regimen or a documented institutional protocol; expressing the total dose as 0.47 mg/kg describes exposure rather than the basis for dose selection. Hematology recommended platelet transfusion according to bleeding and serial blood counts; the transfusion note documented cryoprecipitate for coagulation-factor replacement. Two institutional therapeutic doses of apheresis platelets were transfused from 14:50 to 15:10, with plasma, fibrinogen, and cryoprecipitate support before alteplase. No target platelet or fibrinogen threshold was documented, and no platelet count was obtained after transfusion but before the first infusion. The rise from 21 to 62 × 109/L was interpreted primarily in relation to platelet transfusion, not as spontaneous recovery or an alteplase effect. At 17:00 (t0), UFH was paused and the first 20-mg alteplase infusion began through right lower-limb deep venous access (Figures 1B).
