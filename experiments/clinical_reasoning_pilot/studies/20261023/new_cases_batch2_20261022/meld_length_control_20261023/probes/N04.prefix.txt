A 36-year-old woman presented with a 5-day history of low-grade fever and severe stabbing headaches. Her past medical history was unremarkable. Neurological examination was unremarkable except for marked meningeal signs. A computed tomography (CT) scan of the head revealed no abnormalities. CSF analysis demonstrated inflammatory findings with lymphocytic pleocytosis (183 cells/μL; 97.8% lymphocytes) and elevated protein concentration (83.3 mg/dL). CSF polymerase chain reaction (PCR) testing did not identify an infectious etiology. Serological testing for tick-borne infections, including Borrelia burgdorferi, was negative. Based on the clinical presentation and initial laboratory findings, the patient was initially diagnosed with lymphocytic meningitis syndrome. She received intravenous dexamethasone (Dexaven) for 9 days, starting at 8 mg/day for 2 days, followed by 12 mg/day for 3 days, then tapered to 8 mg/day for 3 days and 4 mg/day on the final day, together with symptomatic treatment. A follow-up lumbar puncture showed partial improvement, with a WBC count of 130 cells/μL and protein concentration of 46.1 mg/dL. After 12 days of hospitalization, she was discharged in good condition on 11 March 2026.
