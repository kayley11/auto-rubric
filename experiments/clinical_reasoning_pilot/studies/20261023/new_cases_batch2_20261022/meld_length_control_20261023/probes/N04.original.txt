A 36-year-old woman presented with a 5-day history of low-grade fever and severe stabbing headaches. Her past medical history was unremarkable. Neurological examination was unremarkable except for marked meningeal signs. A computed tomography (CT) scan of the head revealed no abnormalities. CSF analysis demonstrated inflammatory findings with lymphocytic pleocytosis (183 cells/µL; 97.8% lymphocytes) and elevated protein concentration (83.3 mg/dL). CSF polymerase chain reaction (PCR) testing did not identify an infectious etiology. Serological testing for tick-borne infections, including Borrelia burgdorferi, was negative. Based on the clinical presentation and initial laboratory findings, the patient was initially diagnosed with lymphocytic meningitis syndrome. She received intravenous dexamethasone (Dexaven) for 9 days, starting at 8 mg/day for 2 days, followed by 12 mg/day for 3 days, then tapered to 8 mg/day for 3 days and 4 mg/day on the final day, together with symptomatic treatment. A follow-up lumbar puncture showed partial improvement, with a WBC count of 130 cells/µL and protein concentration of 46.1 mg/dL. After 12 days of hospitalization, she was discharged in good condition on 11 March 2026.

After discharge, the patient continued to experience headache and photophobia, resulting in two visits to the Emergency Department. Head CT performed during these visits showed no abnormalities. On 14 March 2026, three days after the initial discharge, she was readmitted because of severe orthostatic headache accompanied by upper-limb tremor and dizziness. Neurological examination showed neck stiffness and a positive Kernig sign. Brain MRI showed no abnormalities. Contrast-enhanced spinal MRI demonstrated disc herniation at L3–L4 and L4–L5, with possible nerve root compression. CSF analysis showed persistent lymphocytic pleocytosis (178 cells/µL; 97.7% lymphocytes) with a normal protein concentration (36.6 mg/dL).

Samples were sent to the Laboratory of Neuroimmunology, II Department of Neurology, Institute of Psychiatry and Neurology in Warsaw for autoimmune antibody testing. Neuronal surface antibodies were tested using indirect immunofluorescence assay (IIFT) on transfected EU90 cells at a serum/CSF dilution of 1:10. Antibodies against NMDAR, AMPAR1/2, DPPX, LGI1, CASPR2, and GABABR were not detected. GFAP-IgG was also tested using IIFT on transfected EU90 cells at a dilution of 1:10 and was detected in both CSF and serum. In addition, the patient underwent extensive infectious and immunological investigations, including HIV testing, testing for Borrelia burgdorferi and tick-borne encephalitis, Treponema pallidum serology, amphiphysin antibodies, lymphocyte immunophenotyping, and repeated molecular testing for infectious pathogens (Table 1).
