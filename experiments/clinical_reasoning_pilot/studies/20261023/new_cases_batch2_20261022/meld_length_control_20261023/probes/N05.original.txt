A 52-year-old man presented with a 1-year history of persistent watery diarrhoea (3–4 episodes daily), accompanied by acid reflux, upper abdominal discomfort, and numbness in the distal extremities. Previous gastroenterological investigations, including upper gastrointestinal endoscopy, had led to diagnoses of bile reflux gastritis and a colonic lesion treated by endoscopic submucosal dissection. Histopathological examination of the resected lesion showed a sessile serrated lesion with dysplasia. His diarrhoea remained refractory to symptomatic treatment. During the preceding 6 months, he also developed progressive exertional dyspnoea, fatigue, and unintentional weight loss of 10 kg. His medical history included gout and nephrolithiasis. There was no known family history of amyloidosis.

On examination, his blood pressure was 80/46 mmHg and heart rate was 52 beats/min, with no other specific cardiopulmonary or abdominal abnormalities. Baseline NT-proBNP was 887 pg/mL, hs-TnT was 23.9 ng/L, creatinine was 1.3 mg/dL, and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) was 62.7 mL/min/1.73 m2.

Given the bradycardia, hypotension, progressive dyspnoea, and unexplained systemic symptoms, transthoracic echocardiography was performed. This showed symmetrically increased left ventricular wall thickness (interventricular septal of 14.1 mm), reduced LVEF (36%), and grade II diastolic dysfunction. Two-dimensional speckle-tracking analysis demonstrated relative apical sparing on longitudinal strain imaging (Figures 1A,B), raising strong suspicion for cardiac amyloidosis. Cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) imaging reinforced this impression, showing diffuse circumferential left ventricular wall thickening, markedly elevated native myocardial T1 values, extracellular volume values ranging from 49.85% to 52.47%, and diffuse subendocardial late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) affecting the left ventricle and interatrial septum (Figure 1C), findings highly suggestive of cardiac amyloidosis (10).
