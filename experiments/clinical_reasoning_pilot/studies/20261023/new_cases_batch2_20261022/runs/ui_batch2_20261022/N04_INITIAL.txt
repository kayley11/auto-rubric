Autoimmune/immune-mediated meningitis** — Most consistent with the overall pattern: persistent lymphocytic pleocytosis, negative PCR and Borrelia serology, initial improvement with dexamethasone, and relapse after withdrawal. The pending autoimmune antibody panel is the critical next result. Limitation: antibodies are not yet available, and a negative panel would not exclude all autoimmune aetiologies.

**3. Post-procedural CSF leak** — The severe orthostatic headache is characteristic of intracranial hypotension, potentially iatrogenic from lumbar puncture. This would not explain the pleocytosis but may co-exist with an immune-mediated process. The spinal disc herniation is likely incidental and does not account for the CSF or headache pattern.

Before management decisions: confirm whether the orthostatic component reflects a CSF leak (consider contrast-enhanced spinal MRI or CT myelography), await autoimmune antibody results, and reassess whether the pleocytosis is truly persistent or resolving.
