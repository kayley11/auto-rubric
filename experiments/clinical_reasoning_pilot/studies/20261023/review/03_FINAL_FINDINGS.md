# Findings and answers to Josh's questions

Completed: six new cases (eight answers/65 ratings), 18 separate continuation probes, and the latest eight-case, three-model comparison (24 answers/48 ratings). Clinical references and independent clinician ratings remain outstanding.

Staged branches tested **using supplied evidence**; interviews test **actively obtaining it**. N04's antibody branch increased confidence; its neutral branch did not. Interviews exposed omissions hidden by supplying the whole vignette.

## 1. “Do all models behave the same?”

**No, in these runs.** C05 lite/chat/apex obtained 2/4, 4/4 and 3/4 groups. Only apex obtained N02 glucose; all missed C03 shin-lesion history. All three had missed glucose in the first three-model round, so there is run-to-run variation too.

## 2. “Let's see if we get a different response depending on models.”

**Completed with the same cases, rules, six-turn cap and common judge.** Questions, records and final answers differed. Some earlier differences were technical: C02 chat asked for history but was not given it; N05 TTR requests wrongly received GLA. Both were corrected. N04's “that admission” clarification remains a matcher limitation.

## 3. “This would mean it'll score poorly on the problem representation.”

**Not automatically under the current scoring scope.** N02 lite missed glucose but received **D3a=5**; apex obtained glucose and also received **5**. Both received D5c=4.

D5c assesses information gathering using the complete inventory. D3a currently assesses the final representation using only information actually obtained. An answer can summarise its limited evidence well while missing an essential question. The unresolved author decision is **whether, and how, an unelicited essential fact should also lower D3a**. No agreed clinical must-ask list or such penalty has been applied.

D3a applicability to patient/family explanations also needs confirming.
