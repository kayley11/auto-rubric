Working explanations:

1. Post-lumbar puncture CSF leak: The orthostatic headache pattern is classic for intracranial hypotension following LP. Limitation: the headache preceded the LP, and the positional nature before the procedure is unconfirmed. The tremor and dizziness are atypical. Next: confirm true orthostatic pattern, consider MRI brain for meningeal enhancement or sagging brain.

2. Ongoing or recurrent meningitis: The initial CSF showed significant lymphocytic pleocytosis with elevated protein. Persistence of headache and photophobia, plus new neurological signs, raise concern for incomplete resolution or a different aetiology (e.g., autoimmune, viral with atypical course). Limitation: no final diagnosis was documented; dexamethasone for nine days is atypical for simple viral meningitis and its rationale is unclear. Next: repeat LP with cell count, protein, glucose, and targeted PCR panel.

3. Drug-related or post-inflammatory neurological effects: Prolonged dexamethasone can cause tremor and dizziness; however, this does not explain the positional headache.

Reassessment needed before management: confirm orthostatic pattern, repeat CSF, and MRI brain with contrast to distinguish CSF leak from ongoing meningeal inflammation.
