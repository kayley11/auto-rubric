1. One-sentence problem representation

A young adult presenting with persistent bloody diarrhoea, urgency, cramping, morning back stiffness, and a history of erythema nodosum, raising concern for inflammatory bowel disease with extra-intestinal manifestations.

2. Up to three prioritised diagnostic possibilities, with supporting evidence and limitations

Inflammatory bowel disease (ulcerative colitis or Crohn's disease) with associated axial spondyloarthropathy. Supporting evidence: bloody diarrhoea with urgency and cramping in a young adult, prior erythema nodosum (a recognised extra-intestinal manifestation of IBD), and morning back stiffness suggesting inflammatory spondyloarthropathy. Limitation: no colonoscopy or histology to confirm; Crohn's versus ulcerative colitis cannot be distinguished without endoscopy.

Inflammatory spondyloarthropathy with gastrointestinal involvement. Supporting evidence: morning back stiffness and erythema nodosum. Limitation: IBD is a more common cause of this constellation; spondyloarthropathy as a primary diagnosis with GI symptoms is less typical.

Infectious colitis. Supporting evidence: bloody diarrhoea. Limitation: poor response to antibiotics and the presence of extra-intestinal features (back stiffness, prior erythema nodosum) make this less likely, but it has not been excluded.

3. Prioritised investigations with brief purposes

Faecal calprotectin and C-reactive protein: to quantify intestinal and systemic inflammation. Stool culture and Clostridioides difficile testing: to exclude infectious aetiology. Colonoscopy with biopsies: to confirm or exclude IBD and determine subtype. Full blood count, ESR, and HLA-B27: to assess inflammatory burden and spondyloarthropathy association. Pelvic or lumbar spine imaging (MRI): if axial spondyloarthropathy is suspected and clinical examination supports it.

4. Important missing information and the questions you would ask

Duration of diarrhoea and back stiffness. Whether back stiffness improves with movement (inflammatory pattern). Family history of IBD or autoimmune disease. Presence of other joint pain, uveitis, or oral ulcers. Prior investigations or treatments. Exact age and sex.

5. Safety and next steps

Urgent referral to gastroenterology for colonoscopy. If significant anaemia, haemodynamic instability, or severe systemic inflammation is identified, consider inpatient management. Do not initiate immunosuppressive therapy before IBD is confirmed and infection excluded. Monitor for red-flag features: increasing blood loss, fever, or severe dehydration.
