1. Problem representation

An older adult with prior myocardial infarction and a pacemaker presents with orthostatic light-headedness and brief collapses temporally associated with an antihypertensive dose increase.

2. Diagnostic possibilities

- Orthostatic hypotension from antihypertensive over-treatment: supported by the temporal relationship to dose increase; limited by absence of measured blood pressures and an unidentified specific agent.
- Pacemaker-related syncope (capture failure, lead displacement, or mode issue): supported by device presence and recent check; limited by unavailable interrogation result and unknown implant indication.
- Arrhythmic syncope in the context of prior infarction (e.g., ventricular tachycardia): supported by ischaemic substrate; limited by no ECG or device data available.

3. Investigations

- Orthostatic blood pressure measurements (supine and standing): to confirm or exclude orthostatic hypotension.
- Pacemaker interrogation: to assess device function, lead integrity, and arrhythmia burden.
- 12-lead ECG: to evaluate for ischaemic changes, conduction disease, or arrhythmia.
- Medication review: to identify the specific antihypertensive agent and dose.

4. Missing information

- Name and dose of the antihypertensive medicine that was increased.
- Pacemaker indication and result of the recent check.
- Collapse semiology: complete loss of consciousness, duration, prodromal symptoms, recovery pattern.
- Any recent ECG, echocardiogram, or cardiac imaging reports.
- Comorbidities such as diabetes or renal impairment.

5. Safety and next steps

- Advise the patient to avoid driving or operating heavy machinery until the cause of collapse is established.
- Arrange urgent pacemaker interrogation if not yet completed.
- Review the antihypertensive regimen with the prescriber; if orthostatic hypotension is confirmed, consider dose reduction.
- If device-related or arrhythmic cause is identified, refer to electrophysiology or cardiology for further management.
