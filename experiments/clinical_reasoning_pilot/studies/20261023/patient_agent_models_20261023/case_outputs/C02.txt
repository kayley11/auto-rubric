1. Problem representation
A young adult with relapsing neurological symptoms over approximately one year—including optic neuritis, speech difficulty, urinary symptoms, and current gait unsteadiness exacerbated by heat—suggesting a central demyelinating process with dissemination in both time and space.

2. Diagnostic possibilities
Multiple sclerosis (most likely): Dissemination in time (episodes separated by months) and space (optic nerve, brainstem or cerebellum, spinal cord) are present. Worsening with heat (Uhthoff phenomenon) is a recognised feature of demyelination. Limitation: no confirmed MRI or CSF findings are available to support the diagnosis at this stage.
Neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD): Optic neuritis plus spinal cord involvement fits this pattern; however, the relapsing course with partial resolution and the presence of brainstem symptoms are more typical of MS than NMOSD.
MOG antibody disease: A less common demyelinating condition; should be considered if AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG testing is negative and the clinical picture is atypical for MS.

3. Investigations
MRI brain and cervical and thoracic spine with gadolinium contrast: to identify demyelinating lesions and assess dissemination in space and time.
CSF analysis including cell count, protein, oligoclonal bands, and IgG index: to provide supportive evidence for MS.
Serum AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG: to exclude NMOSD and MOG antibody disease.
Visual evoked potentials: to objectively confirm prior optic neuritis.

4. Missing information
Exact age and sex, which affect MS risk and differential diagnosis. Whether MRI has been performed and its results.
