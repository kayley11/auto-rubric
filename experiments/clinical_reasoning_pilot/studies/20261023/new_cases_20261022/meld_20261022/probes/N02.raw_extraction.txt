A 58-year-old female office worker was admitted to the Department of Neurology at the First People’s Hospital of Yunnan Province on June 12, 2026, with a six-month history of nocturnal behavioral abnormalities. The episodes, attributed to chronic work-related stress, occurred repeatedly between 4:00 AM and 6:00 AM and were characterized by behaviors such as folding blankets, standing and walking, wiping tables, shouting, repeatedly touching her head, and thrashing in bed. These episodes were accompanied by urinary incontinence. Family members’ attempts to arouse her during the episodes were ineffective, and she frequently fell out of bed. The patient experienced no choking or awakening during the episodes and had complete amnesia for the events upon waking. She had significant snoring with a loud volume, and family members reported observed apnea episodes. She typically woke spontaneously around 7:00–8:00 AM and felt normal afterward. Since symptom onset, she reported decreased energy and concentration, with a weight loss of approximately 5 kg over the preceding six months.
The patient had a 10-year history of hypertension, managed with regular oral irbesartan. She had been diagnosed with moderate obstructive sleep apnea syndrome one month prior but had not received specific treatment. Family members reported multiple episodes of nocturnal hypoglycemia, with documented blood glucose levels around 2.0 mmol/L. She denied any history of diabetes mellitus, prior surgeries, trauma, or allergies.
Vital signs: temperature 36.5 °C, pulse 83 beats/min, respiratory rate 20/min, blood pressure 142/87 mmHg, BMI 19.5 kg/m². She was alert, with coherent speech and cooperative during examination. Neurological examination: orientation, memory, calculation, and judgment were normal. Speech was fluent with clear articulation. Cranial nerve examination was unremarkable. Muscle strength was grade 5/5 in all limbs, with normal tone and symmetric deep tendon reflexes. Pathological reflexes were absent. Superficial and deep sensation, as well as coordination, were normal. Cardiopulmonary and abdominal examinations revealed no significant abnormalities.
Laboratory Findings: In-hospital nocturnal glucose monitoring revealed frequent hypoglycemic episodes, with levels dropping as low as 1.2–2.0 mmol/L. Concurrent testing demonstrated inappropriately elevated insulin levels: serum C-peptide (fasting) 2.32 ng/mL, insulin (fasting) 9.96 μU/mL, with a significantly elevated insulin-to-glucose ratio, consistent with hyperinsulinemic hypoglycemia. An oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) showed a fasting glucose of 3.8 mmol/L and a 2-hour postprandial glucose of 14.4 mmol/L, indicating impaired glucose tolerance. Serum electrolytes, renal and hepatic function, thyroid function, cortisol rhythm, ACTH, and tumor markers were all within normal limits.
Neurophysiological Examination: Electroencephalography (EEG) performed after admission revealed no definite epileptiform discharges.
