A 58-year-old female office worker was admitted to the Department of Neurology at the First People’s Hospital of Yunnan Province on June 12, 2026, with a six-month history of nocturnal behavioral abnormalities. The episodes, attributed to chronic work-related stress, occurred repeatedly between 4:00 AM and 6:00 AM and were characterized by behaviors such as folding blankets, standing and walking, wiping tables, shouting, repeatedly touching her head, and thrashing in bed. These episodes were accompanied by urinary incontinence. Family members’ attempts to arouse her during the episodes were ineffective, and she frequently fell out of bed. The patient experienced no choking or awakening during the episodes and had complete amnesia for the events upon waking. She had significant snoring with a loud volume, and family members reported observed apnea episodes. She typically woke spontaneously around 7:00–8:00 AM and felt normal afterward. Since symptom onset, she reported decreased energy and concentration, with a weight loss of approximately 5 kg over the preceding six months. The patient had a 10-year history of hypertension, managed with regular oral irbesartan. She had been diagnosed with moderate obstructive sleep apnea syndrome one month prior but had not received specific treatment.
