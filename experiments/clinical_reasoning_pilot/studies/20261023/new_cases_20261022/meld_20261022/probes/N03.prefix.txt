A 54-year-old male was sent to the emergency department (ED) within 30 min of being found unresponsive at a health spa. His medical history was limited to treated hepatitis C and a remote appendectomy, with no known chronic illnesses or regular medication use. Upon arrival, his vital signs were notable for relative hypotension (104/58 mmHg), bradypnea (12 breaths/min), and hypothermia (34.3 °C), with a Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) score of E3V2M5. Physical examination revealed a state of flaccid paralysis with generalized areflexia, including absent deep tendon reflexes. Laboratory screening for common drugs of abuse was negative. Initial blood samples obtained after ED arrival showed blood urea nitrogen and serum creatinine concentrations of 14 and 0.9 mg/dL, respectively.
