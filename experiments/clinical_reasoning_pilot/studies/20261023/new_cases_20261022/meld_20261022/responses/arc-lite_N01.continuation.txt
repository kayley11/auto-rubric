The hyponatremia was managed with fluid restriction to 1.0 L/day and intravenous 3% hypertonic saline administered at 0.5 mL/kg/h for 6 hours, which raised serum sodium to 124.1 mEq/L over 24 hours. The rate of correction was monitored every 4 hours to avoid osmotic demyelination, and hypertonic saline was discontinued once serum sodium exceeded 125 mEq/L. Oral sodium chloride tablets (1 g every 8 hours) were added as a bridge to maintain serum sodium above 130 mEq/L. Desmopressin was temporarily held for 48 hours, after which it was resumed at a reduced dose of 180 μg/day in consultation with endocrinology, given the concern that the prior dose may have contributed to the degree of water retention. Cisplatin, a well-recognized cause of nephrogenic diabetes insipidus and, paradoxically, of SIADH-like presentations, was considered a potential contributor to the electrolyte derangement, although the temporal relationship and the pre-existing SIADH phenotype made a multifactorial etiology most likely. ICE cycle 2 was delayed by 14 days to allow full recovery of serum sodium to 138.6 mEq/L and to stabilize the endocrine regimen.
