On admission, physical examination revealed a blood pressure of 145/90 mmHg, a pulse of 78 beats per minute, and a body mass index of 27.8 kg/m2. Neurological examination was unremarkable, with no focal deficits, and the mental status examination was normal. Routine blood tests, including complete blood count, liver and renal function, electrolytes, and thyroid function, were within normal limits. A fasting plasma glucose of 3.1 mmol/L was noted, with an HbA1c of 5.2%. An overnight polysomnography study confirmed moderate obstructive sleep apnea with an apnea–hypopnea index of 22 events per hour. Prolonged video-electroencephalography captured two typical episodes arising from non-rapid eye movement sleep, without epileptiform discharges. Abdominal ultrasound and pancreatic computed tomography revealed a 1.8 cm mass in the pancreatic tail, suggestive of an insulinoma. Endoscopic ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration was subsequently performed, and histopathological examination confirmed the diagnosis of a well-differentiated pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor. The patient was referred to the Department of Hepatobiliary Surgery for surgical management.
