Given the constellation of descending flaccid paralysis, areflexia, and respiratory compromise in the absence of a toxicological explanation, botulism was strongly suspected. The patient was intubated for airway protection and mechanical ventilation. Stool and serum samples were sent for botulinum toxin testing. He was admitted to the intensive care unit and received heptavalent botulinum antitoxin. Over the following days, his respiratory function gradually improved, and he was extubated on day 4. Full neurological recovery was observed over several weeks, and toxin testing confirmed the diagnosis of foodborne botulism.
