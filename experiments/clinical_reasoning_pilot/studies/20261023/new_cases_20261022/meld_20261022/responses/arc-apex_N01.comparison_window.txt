Output volumes were 2.5–3.5 L/day, with spot urinary sodium of 84.2 mEq/L and urine osmolality of 512 mOsm/kg, consistent with an ongoing sodium-wasting pattern rather than dilutional hyponatremia. Hypertonic saline was not required; instead, oral sodium chloride tablets were initiated and up-titrated, fluid intake was liberalized rather than restricted, and desmopressin was continued at the established dose, given the presumed combined central diabetes insipidus and cerebral salt-wasting physiology. Serum sodium corrected gradually to 132 mEq/L by cycle 1 day 14 and normalized before cycle 2. Hydrocortisone was empirically increased to 50 mg/day during the nadir, then returned to 30 mg/day after clinical improvement, without evidence of adrenal crisis. ICE cycle 2 proceeded at full doses on schedule. During cycle 2, serum sodium declined again to a nadir of 124.6 mEq/L on day 8, with similar urinary sodium and osmolality findings, and again responded to intensified oral sodium supplementation without hypertonic saline or desmopressin adjustment. Cycles 3 and 4 were complicated by milder hyponatremia (nadir 130–133 mEq/L), managed with continued oral sodium chloride and stable endocrine replacement. The patient completed all four planned ICE cycles without dose reduction or treatment delay attributable to sodium disturbances. Restaging imaging after completion of salvage chemotherapy showed no evidence of recurrent disease, and the patient remained in remission
