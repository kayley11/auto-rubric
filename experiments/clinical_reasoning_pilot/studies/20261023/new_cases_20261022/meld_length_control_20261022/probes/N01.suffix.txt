At the sodium nadir, spot urinary sodium was 22.8 mEq/L and urine osmolality was 176 mOsm/kg; subsequent spot values varied from 13.4 to 232.9 mEq/L, and urine osmolality varied from 75 to 639 mOsm/kg (Fig. 3b). The patient received 3% hypertonic saline, isotonic fluids, and oral salt supplementation, with frequent serum sodium, urine-output, and urinary-index monitoring. Serum sodium subsequently rose to 136.3 mEq/L on day 12 and 142.8 mEq/L on day 16. The available body-weight record did not show a clinically important change during the sodium disturbances. Rechallenge during ICE cycle 2 reproduced the high-output pattern (Fig. 3b). Recorded urine output rose from 1.40 L/day on cycle 2 day 0 to 4.25 L/day on day 3 and 9.75 L/day on day 4. Spot urinary sodium concentrations were 241.8 and 196.2 mEq/L, with urine osmolalities of 617 and 541 mOsm/kg, on days 3 and 4, respectively. Serum sodium was 124.4 mEq/L on day 3 and 123.3 mEq/L on day 4. These serial findings indicated a chemotherapy-associated mixed sodium–water disturbance with a suspected salt-wasting component. Cisplatin and ifosfamide were discontinued after cycle 2 day 3; etoposide was continued on days 4–5. By day 17, serum sodium was 143.9 mEq/L and measured serum osmolality was 288 mOsm/kg. This recovery did not identify one mechanism because drug exposure, intake, infused fluids, salt supplementation, and water handling changed concurrently.
