A 54-year-old male was sent to the emergency department (ED) within 30 min of being found unresponsive at a health spa. His medical history was limited to treated hepatitis C and a remote appendectomy, with no known chronic illnesses or regular medication use. Upon arrival, his vital signs were notable for relative hypotension (104/58 mmHg), bradypnea (12 breaths/min), and hypothermia (34.3 °C), with a Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) score of E3V2M5. Physical examination revealed a state of flaccid paralysis with generalized areflexia, including absent deep tendon reflexes.
Laboratory screening for common drugs of abuse was negative. Initial blood samples obtained after ED arrival showed blood urea nitrogen and serum creatinine concentrations of 14 and 0.9 mg/dL, respectively. Arterial blood gas analysis demonstrated mixed respiratory and metabolic acidosis, with a pH of 7.183, PaCO2 of 50.1 mmHg, and bicarbonate concentration of 18.4 mEq/L. Additional findings included a serum K+ concentration of 4.8 mmol/L, hyperchloremia of 116 mmol/L, an elevated measured serum osmolality of 324 mOsm/kg, a lactate concentration of 3.7 mmol/L, and a hemoglobin concentration of 18.1 g/dL. Serum Mg2+ was critically elevated at 15.8 mg/dL (reference range, 1.7–2.4 mg/dL). For the serial timeline, the time at which the initial laboratory results became available following ED arrival was designated as index time 0. The initial 12-lead ECG demonstrated sinus rhythm with prominent T waves and diffuse ST-segment depression (Figure 1A), and AI-ECG analysis identified a high estimated mortality risk (>99th percentile) (Lin et al., 2024). Non-contrast brain computed tomography was unremarkable.
