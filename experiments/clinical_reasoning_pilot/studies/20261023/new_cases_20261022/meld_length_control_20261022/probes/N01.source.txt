Curative-intent ifosfamide, cisplatin, and etoposide (ICE) was started because of the prolonged remission, biopsy-proven recurrence, and anticipated chemosensitivity. The planned 28-day regimen comprised ifosfamide 900 mg/m2, cisplatin 20 mg/m2, and etoposide 100 mg/m2 on days 1–5. Scheduled oral desmopressin 360 μg/day, hydrocortisone 30 mg/day, and levothyroxine 50 μg/day were continued during the sodium disturbances following endocrinology consultation, which supported maintenance of the established doses and schedules. Before salvage chemotherapy, serum sodium was 139.3 mEq/L, measured serum osmolality was 278 mOsm/kg, serum creatinine was 0.52 mg/dL, estimated glomerular filtration rate was 163.4 mL/min/1.73 m2, spot urinary sodium was 91.4 mEq/L, and urine osmolality was 555 mOsm/kg. Baseline cortisol was reported as 5.5 μg/dL, but sampling time relative to hydrocortisone was unavailable, and the value was not used to infer adrenal reserve. Baseline laboratory results are provided in Online Resource 3. The course of serum sodium and serum osmolality, oral sodium supplementation, treatment modifications, and urinary sodium/osmolality trends during ICE are shown in Fig. 3a, b. After ICE cycle 1, fatigue, anorexia, and headache accompanied grade 4 hypotonic hyponatremia (Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events [CTCAE] version 5.0): serum sodium reached 118.4 mEq/L on cycle 1 day 9, and measured serum osmolality was 236 mOsm/kg. Recorded daily urine output during cycle 1 ranged from 0.768 to 2.489 L/day. At the sodium nadir, spot urinary sodium was 22.8 mEq/L and urine osmolality was 176 mOsm/kg; subsequent spot values varied from 13.4 to 232.9 mEq/L, and urine osmolality varied from 75 to 639 mOsm/kg (Fig. 3b). The patient received 3% hypertonic saline, isotonic fluids, and oral salt supplementation, with frequent serum sodium, urine-output, and urinary-index monitoring. Serum sodium subsequently rose to 136.3 mEq/L on day 12 and 142.8 mEq/L on day 16. The available body-weight record did not show a clinically important change during the sodium disturbances. Rechallenge during ICE cycle 2 reproduced the high-output pattern (Fig. 3b). Recorded urine output rose from 1.40 L/day on cycle 2 day 0 to 4.25 L/day on day 3 and 9.75 L/day on day 4. Spot urinary sodium concentrations were 241.8 and 196.2 mEq/L, with urine osmolalities of 617 and 541 mOsm/kg, on days 3 and 4, respectively. Serum sodium was 124.4 mEq/L on day 3 and 123.3 mEq/L on day 4. These serial findings indicated a chemotherapy-associated mixed sodium–water disturbance with a suspected salt-wasting component. Cisplatin and ifosfamide were discontinued after cycle 2 day 3; etoposide was continued on days 4–5. By day 17, serum sodium was 143.9 mEq/L and measured serum osmolality was 288 mOsm/kg. This recovery did not identify one mechanism because drug exposure, intake, infused fluids, salt supplementation, and water handling changed concurrently.
