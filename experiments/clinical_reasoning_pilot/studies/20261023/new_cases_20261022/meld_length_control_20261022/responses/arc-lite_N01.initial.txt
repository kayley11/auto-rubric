The clinical picture, temporal relationship to ifosfamide exposure, and exclusion of hypovolemia, hypothyroidism, and glucocorticoid deficiency (given stable replacement doses) supported ifosfamide-induced SIADH as the most likely etiology. Management included fluid restriction to 1.0 L/day, intravenous 3% hypertonic saline at 100 mL/h for 6 hours targeting a serum sodium correction of no more than 8 mEq/L per 24 hours, and close monitoring with serum sodium measurements every 4–6 hours. Serum sodium rose to 124.1 mEq/L by day 12 and 131.2 mEq/L by day 15, at which point fluid restriction was relaxed to 1.5 L/day and oral sodium supplementation (NaCl 1 g three times daily) was initiated. By day 18, serum sodium had normalized to 136.8 mEq/L and remained stable thereafter. For cycle 2, the ifosfamide dose was reduced by 25% to 675 mg/m2, and prophylactic fluid restriction to 1.5 L/day was instituted from day 1 through day 10. Serum nadir sodium during cycle 2 was 131.4 mEq/L on day 8, which was managed with oral sodium supplementation alone without the need for hypertonic saline. Cycles 3 and 4 were completed with the same dose modification and monitoring protocol, with no episode of hyponatremia below 130 mEq/L. The patient achieved a complete metabolic response by cycle 4, confirmed by repeat imaging and normalization of tumor markers. At 6-month follow-up, the patient remained in complete remission with no evidence of recurrence. Serum sodium, osmolality, and renal function had returned to baseline values. The case underscores the importance of proactive monitoring and early intervention in ifosfamide-associated hyponatremia, particularly in patients with pre-existing sodium-wasting tendencies or concurrent medications that may compound the risk. The combination of desmopressin, which can potentiate water retention, and ifosfamide-induced SIADH likely contributed to the severity of the initial episode, highlighting the need for individualized risk assessment before initiating platinum-based salvage regimens in patients with complex endocrine histories.
