Fluid balance charts documented a cumulative positive balance of approximately 2.5 L over the first week despite a target intake restriction of 1–1.5 L/day, which the patient could not fully adhere to because of thirst. Serum potassium, magnesium, and phosphate were within normal limits, and renal function remained stable, arguing against a renal salt-wasting syndrome driven by tubular injury. Serum cortisol drawn 12 h after the last hydrocortisone dose was 3.1 μg/dL, and hydrocortisone was empirically doubled to 60 mg/day during the acute illness; thyroid function tests were unchanged. Hyponatremia was managed with a combination of strict fluid restriction, temporary discontinuation of desmopressin for 24 h followed by dose reduction to 240 μg/day, and cautious oral sodium chloride supplementation starting at 3 g/day. Serum sodium corrected by 6 mEq/L over the first 48 h and returned to 132 mEq/L by cycle 1 day 16. Neurological symptoms resolved completely, and no seizures or other serious complications occurred. Because of the severity of the episode, cycle 2 was delayed by 1 week and ifosfamide was reduced by 25% to 675 mg/m2, while cisplatin and etoposide doses were unchanged.
