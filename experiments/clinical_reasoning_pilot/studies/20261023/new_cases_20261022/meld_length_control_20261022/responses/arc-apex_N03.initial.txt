Given the combination of unexplained coma, hypothermia, bradypnea, and flaccid areflexic paralysis, toxic ingestion was strongly suspected despite the negative initial screen. The patient was intubated for airway protection and started on empirical supportive therapy, including active rewarming with warmed intravenous fluids and a forced diuresis protocol. A collateral history obtained from a family member revealed that the patient had recently obtained botulinum toxin and colchicine through an online source for cosmetic purposes, raising concern for iatrogenic botulism. A serum sample was therefore sent for botulinum toxin testing, and the patient was treated empirically with heptavalent botulinum antitoxin. Over the following 48 hours, however, he failed to improve; pupils remained midposition and reactive, and repeat neurologic examinations continued to show profound flaccid weakness. Additional history from a coworker ultimately disclosed that the patient had been consuming an herbal preparation containing autumn crocus (Colchicum autumnale) leaves, which he had mistakenly foraged and brewed as a tea in the days preceding presentation. Colchicine toxicity was suspected, and this was later confirmed by measurement of a serum colchicine concentration of 12 ng/mL approximately 72 hours after ingestion. Supportive care was intensified with aggressive fluid resuscitation, broad-spectrum antibiotics for aspiration pneumonia, and monitoring for the anticipated hematologic complications.
