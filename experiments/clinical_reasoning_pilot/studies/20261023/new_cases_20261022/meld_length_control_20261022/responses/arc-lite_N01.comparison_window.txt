The clinical picture, temporal relationship to ifosfamide exposure, and exclusion of hypovolemia, hypothyroidism, and glucocorticoid deficiency (given stable replacement doses) supported ifosfamide-induced SIADH as the most likely etiology. Management included fluid restriction to 1.0 L/day, intravenous 3% hypertonic saline at 100 mL/h for 6 hours targeting a serum sodium correction of no more than 8 mEq/L per 24 hours, and close monitoring with serum sodium measurements every 4–6 hours. Serum sodium rose to 124.1 mEq/L by day 12 and 131.2 mEq/L by day 15, at which point fluid restriction was relaxed to 1.5 L/day and oral sodium supplementation (NaCl 1 g three times daily) was initiated. By day 18, serum sodium had normalized to 136.8 mEq/L and remained stable thereafter. For cycle 2, the ifosfamide dose was reduced by 25% to 675 mg/m2, and prophylactic fluid restriction to 1.5 L/day was instituted from day 1 through day 10. Serum nadir sodium during cycle 2 was 131.4 mEq/L on day 8, which was managed with oral sodium supplementation alone without the need for hypertonic saline.
