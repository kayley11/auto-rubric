N03 — Altered consciousness and an abnormal magnesium result

CASE FACTS
A 54-year-old man is brought to the emergency department within 30 minutes of being found unresponsive at a health spa. His recorded history includes treated hepatitis C and a remote appendectomy; there is no known chronic kidney disease or regular medicine use.

On arrival: blood pressure 104/58 mmHg, respiratory rate 12/min, temperature 34.3°C and Glasgow Coma Scale E3 V2 M5 (total 10). He has flaccid weakness and generalised areflexia.

Initial results: magnesium 15.8 mg/dL (laboratory reference 1.7–2.4), creatinine 0.9 mg/dL, blood urea nitrogen 14 mg/dL, potassium 4.8 mmol/L, arterial pH 7.183, PaCO2 50.1 mmHg and bicarbonate 18.4 mEq/L. ECG shows sinus rhythm, prominent T waves and diffuse ST depression. Non-contrast brain CT is unremarkable; screening for common drugs of abuse is negative.

The decision point is when these initial laboratory results become available after arrival, before treatment. A detailed exposure history, product information and subsequent clinical course are not supplied.

MODEL ANSWER
**Immediate priorities:** Secure the airway (GCS 10 with flaccid weakness and areflexia places him at high aspiration risk), initiate continuous cardiac monitoring, and administer IV calcium to antagonize the neuromuscular and cardiac effects of the established severe hypermagnesemia (15.8 mg/dL, approximately six times the upper reference limit). The normal creatinine and BUN confirm intact renal function, supporting consideration of IV loop diuretics to promote renal magnesium excretion. Escalate to ICU-level care and request nephrology consultation early; hemodialysis should be available if clinical status does not improve.

The ECG changes (prominent T waves, diffuse ST depression) and mixed acid-base derangement (pH 7.18, PaCO₂ 50, HCO₃ 18) are consistent with the degree of hypermagnesemia and do not by themselves confirm a specific toxin. The unconfirmed cause remains: the mechanism of magnesium exposure is unknown. The most useful missing history is what occurred at the health spa—whether he received a magnesium-containing bath, enema, topical preparation, or intravenous infusion—and whether he ingested any magnesium supplement or antacid. Do not delay calcium and airway management while awaiting this history.
