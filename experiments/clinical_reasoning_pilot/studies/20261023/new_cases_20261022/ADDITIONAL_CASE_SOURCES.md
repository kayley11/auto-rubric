## Candidate 1 GFAP astrocytopathy

**Trojan et al., Frontiers in Immunology, 16 October 2026.** [When encephalitis is not an infection](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2026.1960742/full). DOI: 10.3389/fimmu.2026.1960742. Source location: Case descriptions, Case 1; Table 1.

A 36-year-old woman had persistent/recurrent headache and inflammatory CSF despite negative infectious investigations and normal brain MRI. Later testing detected GFAP-IgG in CSF and serum. The article also reports a second patient.

**Proposed task:** use Case 1 initially, withholding the antibody result. Ask for alternatives and useful next investigations; then reveal the reported result and ask what changes. This can exercise belief updating and uncertainty. Josh should confirm acceptable differentials and how much diagnostic certainty the result warrants. Keep subsequent treatment and outcome out of the initial task.

## Candidate 2 Multisystem transthyretin amyloidosis

**Cai et al., Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine, 14 October 2026.** [Gastrointestinal-onset hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis with equivocal scintigraphy](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cardiovascular-medicine/articles/10.3389/fcvm.2026.1928392/full). DOI: 10.3389/fcvm.2026.1928392. Source location: Methods and results, clinical presentation, imaging and biopsy sections.

A 52-year-old man had diarrhoea, weight loss, sensory symptoms and cardiomyopathy. PYP scintigraphy was equivocal; later tissue and genetic investigations supported the reported diagnosis.

**Proposed task:** ask how to combine the multisystem findings and interpret the equivocal result before revealing subtyping/genetics. This tests evidence integration and calibrated next steps. Josh should confirm what can be inferred at each stage and defensible investigation alternatives. Do not require the rare variant or treat one published diagnostic pathway as mandatory.

## Candidate 3 Pulmonary embolism with competing bleeding risk

**Kang et al., Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine, 8 October 2026.** [Staged alteplase under VA-ECMO for pulmonary embolism with severe thrombocytopenia](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cardiovascular-medicine/articles/10.3389/fcvm.2026.1977983/full). DOI: 10.3389/fcvm.2026.1977983. Source location: Case description; Table 1 and its timing notes.

A 28-year-old man had obstructive shock and severe thrombocytopenia. The report distinguishes sampling, result reporting and retrospectively derived treatment times. For example, a 20:13 coagulation sample was reported at 21:06.

**Proposed task:** assess emergency escalation, bleeding-risk discussion and which evidence was available for a stated decision. Preserve derived times as estimates. Josh should define acceptable management alternatives and safety requirements. The article explicitly presents an individualised course, not a validated dosing regimen; copying that regimen should not be the required answer.

## Candidate 4 Progressive neurological decline after a stroke diagnosis

**Hao et al., Frontiers in Cellular Neuroscience, 16 October 2026.** [Sporadic CJD initially misdiagnosed as ischaemic stroke](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cellular-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fncel.2026.1938627/full). DOI: 10.3389/fncel.2026.1938627. Source location: Case presentation; Table 1, diagnostic turning points.

A 76-year-old man had vascular abnormalities but subsequently developed progressive cognitive and neurological decline. Serial imaging and CSF findings prompted a revised diagnosis. The authors classified it as probable sCJD; RT-QuIC and neuropathological confirmation were unavailable.

**Proposed task:** reveal the documented progression in stages and ask when the original explanation becomes insufficient. This tests updating without ignoring real vascular findings. Josh should confirm acceptable competing diagnoses and certainty wording. The reference must retain “probable”, rather than score a definite diagnosis as established.

## Selection and remaining checks

Candidates 1–3 would add three distinct tasks: diagnostic updating, equivocal-test interpretation and emergency/time-sensitive risk assessment. Candidate 4 is a useful reserve for updating and uncertainty. These are task-design recommendations, not clinical endorsements of every statement in the reports.

Before using a candidate, fix the clinical decision point, separate available facts from later findings, preserve the original source passage, and obtain clinical reference review. Repeat any exposure checks as a separate experiment. The bounded search used each DOI plus `preprint`; it did not establish the earliest public appearance.
