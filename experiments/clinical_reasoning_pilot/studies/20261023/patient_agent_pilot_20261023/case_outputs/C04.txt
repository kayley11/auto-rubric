1. Problem representation

An older adult with prior myocardial infarction and a pacemaker presents with postural light-headedness and brief collapses following an antihypertensive dose increase.

2. Diagnostic possibilities

- Orthostatic hypotension secondary to antihypertensive dose increase: supported by temporal relationship to dose change and postural symptoms. Limitation: no documented orthostatic blood pressure measurements.
- Pacemaker malfunction or inappropriate pacing: supported by presence of a pacemaker and syncope. Limitation: pacemaker was recently checked but timing and findings are unspecified.
- Arrhythmia related to prior myocardial infarction: supported by cardiac history. Limitation: no ECG or rhythm data provided.

3. Investigations

- Orthostatic blood pressure and heart rate measurements: to confirm or exclude orthostatic hypotension.
- 12-lead ECG: to assess for ischaemic changes, conduction abnormalities, or arrhythmia.
- Pacemaker interrogation: to evaluate lead function, battery status, and stored arrhythmia events.
- Basic metabolic panel and haemoglobin: to exclude contributing metabolic or anaemic causes.

4. Missing information

- Which antihypertensive was increased, by how much, and when relative to symptom onset.
- Timing and findings of the recent pacemaker check.
- Character of collapses: loss of consciousness duration, prodrome, recovery.
- Current medication list including beta-blockers, diuretics, or rate-control agents.
- Any chest pain, palpitations, or dyspnoea accompanying episodes.
- Baseline blood pressure and heart rate prior to the dose change.

5. Safety and next steps

- Advise the patient to avoid driving and to sit or lie down immediately upon feeling light-headed.
- Given the patient lives alone, arrange a home safety review and consider temporary supervision or a fall-prevention plan.
- Urgent cardiology review is warranted to correlate symptoms with pacemaker data and ECG before any further medication adjustment.
- If syncope recurs or is prolonged, emergency assessment is indicated.
