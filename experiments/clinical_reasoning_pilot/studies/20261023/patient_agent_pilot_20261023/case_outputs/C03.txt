1. Problem representation

A young adult with post-travel persistent diarrhoea, bloody stools, urgency, cramps, limited antibiotic response, morning spinal stiffness, and prior painful shin lesions, without reported weight loss or fever.

2. Prioritised diagnostic possibilities

- Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), particularly ulcerative colitis: bloody diarrhoea, urgency, cramps, and morning stiffness (suggesting axial spondyloarthritis) are consistent. Limitation: no confirmed diagnosis; travel timing may be coincidental.
- Post-infectious IBS or persistent infectious colitis: travel history supports an infectious trigger. Limitation: blood and systemic features (shin lesions, stiffness) are atypical for simple post-infectious IBS.
- Reactive arthritis with extra-articular manifestations: painful shin lesions (erythema nodosum) and morning stiffness fit, but diarrhoea with blood is less typical as a primary presentation.

3. Prioritised investigations

- Stool studies (culture, C. difficile, ova/parasites, calprotectin): to exclude active infection and quantify intestinal inflammation.
- Fecal calprotectin and inflammatory markers (CRP, ESR): to assess degree of mucosal inflammation.
- Colonoscopy with biopsies: to confirm or exclude IBD and identify histological pattern.
- HLA-B27 and pelvic/spine imaging: if axial spondyloarthritis is suspected.

4. Missing information and questions

- Duration and exact onset relative to travel; destination and food/water exposure.
- Character of shin lesions (morphology, distribution, timing).
- Joint symptoms: which joints, duration of morning stiffness, response to NSAIDs.
- Family history of IBD or spondyloarthritis.
- Prior stool or blood test results.
- Current medications and antibiotic details.

5. Safety and next steps

- If bloody diarrhoea is ongoing, urgent GI referral is warranted to exclude IBD or severe colitis.
- If fever, significant weight loss, or dehydration develop, reassess acuity.
- Do not delay colonoscopy if IBD is suspected; early confirmation guides management.
- Monitor for red flags: high-volume bleeding, toxic megacolon signs, or systemic deterioration.
