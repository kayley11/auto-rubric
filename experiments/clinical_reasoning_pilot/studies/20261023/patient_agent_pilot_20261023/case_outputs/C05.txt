**What may have happened**

You have had two episodes that fit the pattern of generalised seizures — meaning the whole brain was briefly affected, causing loss of awareness and involuntary movements. Two episodes within three months is enough for a specialist to consider a diagnosis of epilepsy, though this is not a final label yet.

**What the tests show — and what they don't**

Your CT scan of the head was normal, which is reassuring: it means there is no obvious structural cause such as a tumour, bleed, or large area of scarring. Metabolic causes (like abnormal blood sugar, calcium, or sodium levels) have also been excluded. However, a normal CT does not rule out epilepsy. It simply tells us the cause is not something visible on that particular scan. A brain MRI and an EEG (a test that records brain electrical activity) may be arranged to look more closely.

**What happens next**

You will be seen by a neurologist or epilepsy specialist. They may discuss whether a preventive medication is appropriate for you. This is a shared decision — you will be involved in weighing benefits and side effects.

**Practical safety**

Until your specialist advises otherwise, it is wise to avoid swimming alone, working at heights, or being in situations where a sudden loss of awareness could cause serious injury. Regarding driving, you must follow your local licensing rules and speak with your treating team about when you may be eligible to drive again.

**What I would like to understand**

I want to make sure your priorities guide our plan. For example, how do these episodes affect your modelling work, your daily routine, or your sense of safety?

**Two questions for you**

1. Have you noticed any warning sensations — such as a strange smell, feeling, or brief confusion — before either episode?
