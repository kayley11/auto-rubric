1. Problem representation
Young adult with relapsing neurological deficits in different central nervous system regions over twelve months, with heat-exacerbated gait ataxia, raising suspicion for a demyelinating disease.

2. Prioritised diagnostic possibilities
- Multiple sclerosis (most likely): dissemination in time (painful unilateral blurred vision with urinary symptoms months ago; speech disturbance a year ago; gait imbalance now) and in space (optic nerve, brainstem or cerebellum, spinal cord). Heat-exacerbated worsening (Uhthoff phenomenon) supports demyelination. Limitation: no confirmed MRI or CSF findings yet.
- Neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder: can present with optic neuritis and myelitis; however, multi-region relapsing pattern is less typical. Limitation: AQP4-IgG status unknown.
- Other mimics (sarcoidosis, CNS vasculitis, infection): less likely given relapsing-remitting course and young age, but must be excluded.

3. Prioritised investigations
- MRI brain and spine with gadolinium: identify disseminated lesions, active inflammation, and exclude mimics.
- CSF analysis (cell count, protein, oligoclonal bands, IgG index): supports MS diagnosis.
- AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG serology: exclude NMOSD and MOG-antibody disease.
- Visual evoked potentials: confirm subclinical optic nerve demyelination.
- Bloods (FBC, ESR, CRP, B12, ANA, ACE, HIV, syphilis serology): exclude mimics.

4. Important missing information
- Exact duration and severity of each episode; any sensory level or weakness during urinary symptoms.
- Whether the speech disturbance was dysarthria or dysphasia.
- Any prior imaging or CSF results.
- Family history of autoimmune disease.
- Current medications.

5. Safety and next steps
- Urgent neurology referral for definitive diagnosis.
- If an acute relapse is confirmed, discuss corticosteroid therapy with the treating team; no drug is prescribed here.
- Advise avoiding heat exposure pending diagnosis.
- Do not drive if gait is impaired.
- Monitor for new or worsening neurological deficits.
