- Cardiac ischaemia or structural heart disease: A prior MI increases the risk of recurrent ischaemia, heart failure, or valvular pathology causing syncope. Limitation: no ECG, echocardiogram, or symptom detail (chest pain, palpitations) is recorded to support or exclude this.

3. Prioritised investigations

- Orthostatic blood pressure measurement (supine and standing at 1 and 3 minutes): to confirm or exclude orthostatic hypotension.
- Pacemaker interrogation: to assess device function, battery status, and any recorded arrhythmias.
- 12-lead ECG: to identify conduction abnormalities, ischaemic changes, or arrhythmia.
- Consider echocardiogram if structural heart disease is suspected after the above.

4. Important missing information

- Any recorded blood pressure readings, standing or lying.
- Frequency and circumstances of the collapses since the dose change.

5. Safety and next steps

- Advise the patient to avoid driving or operating machinery until the cause of the collapses is clarified.
- Arrange urgent orthostatic blood pressure measurement and pacemaker interrogation.
