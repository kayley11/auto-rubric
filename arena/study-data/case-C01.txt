2. NSAID-induced minimal change disease: Intermittent NSAID use is a well-recognised cause of nephrotic syndrome. Frothy urine and generalised oedema support significant proteinuria. AA amyloidosis secondary to rheumatoid arthritis: Chronic inflammatory disease can lead to secondary amyloid deposition in the kidney, producing nephrotic-range proteinuria. Limitation: duration of RA and prior inflammatory burden are unknown. Other primary glomerular disease such as membranous nephropathy: Cannot be excluded without further workup.

3. Urine dipstick and urine protein-to-creatinine ratio to quantify proteinuria and confirm nephrotic range. Serum albumin, creatinine, and electrolytes to assess hypoalbuminaemia and renal function. Blood pressure measurement to screen for hypertensive renal disease. If proteinuria is confirmed, renal ultrasound and referral to nephrology for possible renal biopsy.

4. Duration of swelling and RA; severity and duration of NSAID use. Any prior urine or blood test results. Blood pressure history. Weight changes, dietary salt intake, and other medications. Whether the patient has had any urinalysis in the past.

5. Advise the patient to reduce or stop NSAID use pending evaluation, as continued use may worsen renal injury. Arrange urgent bloods including full blood count, urea and electrolytes, albumin, and liver function tests, plus a urine sample for dipstick and protein-to-creatinine ratio. If nephrotic-range proteinuria is confirmed, refer to nephrology for further management including possible biopsy.
