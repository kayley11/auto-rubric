Inflammatory bowel disease (ulcerative colitis or Crohn's disease) with associated axial spondyloarthritis: The combination of bloody diarrhoea with urgency and cramping in a young adult is highly suggestive of IBD. Morning back stiffness is characteristic of inflammatory (not mechanical) back pain, and axial spondyloarthritis is a well-recognised extra-intestinal manifestation of IBD. Limitation: no confirmed diagnosis; duration and family history unknown.

Inflammatory bowel disease alone with coincidental mechanical back pain: Possible, but morning stiffness pattern favours inflammatory aetiology. Limitation: no joint examination or imaging performed.

Infectious colitis: Less likely given poor antibiotic response, but not excluded without stool studies. Limitation: no stool culture or calprotectin available.

3. Prioritised investigations

Faecal calprotectin: to confirm intestinal inflammation and support IBD over functional or infectious causes.

Stool culture and C. difficile toxin: to exclude infectious aetiology before committing to IBD workup.

Colonoscopy with biopsies: gold standard to confirm IBD, determine subtype, and assess severity.

HLA-B27 and pelvic/spine MRI: to evaluate for sacroiliitis and confirm axial spondyloarthritis.

FBC, CRP, ESR: baseline inflammatory markers.

4. Important missing information and questions

Any prior investigations (bloods, imaging, endoscopy).

Any eye redness or light sensitivity (uveitis).
