# JA05 — Encephalopathy immediately after anaesthesia


Task

Write 180–240 words for a clinician. Reframe the postoperative neurological problem, compare important categories of explanation and state immediate specialist assessment and information priorities. Use the anaesthetic exposure, lactate and reported imaging pattern without assuming temporal association proves a particular mechanism. Do not invent an intraoperative oxygenation event, a genetic result, maternal ancestry or a later response, and do not prescribe an anaesthetic regimen.


Case input

A previously healthy 35-year-old Venezuelan man undergoes repair of a traumatic open-globe injury. Before surgery, no neurological deficit or evidence of traumatic brain injury was described. Anaesthesia includes inhaled sevoflurane. At the end of surgery he cannot be extubated because of an acute encephalopathy. He is confused and agitated, does not follow commands and withdraws symmetrically to pain. A new lactate of 11.6 mmol/L is recorded. The report describes no electrolyte abnormality or intoxication on the serum and urine tests performed; the scope of those tests is not specified. MRI describes bilateral globus pallidus and subcortical white-matter diffusion restriction, with frontal and temporo-occipital predominance, corresponding FLAIR change and no haemorrhage or abnormal enhancement. Maternal ancestry, genetic testing and subsequent recovery are not supplied.
