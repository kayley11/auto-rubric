*   **Synchronous Primary Renal Cell Carcinoma (RCC):** Supported by the size and infiltrative nature of the renal mass and the presence of renal vein thrombosis, which is a classic feature of RCC. However, this does not explain the rising AFP or the new pulmonary nodules.
*   **Complicated Pyelonephritis/Renal Infarction:** Initially considered due to perirenal oedema and bacteria in the urine. However, the development of a 9 cm mass and pulmonary nodules makes a purely infectious or bland thrombotic process unlikely.

### 3. Timeline and Evidence Limits
The sequence began with a right orchiectomy for mixed GCT, followed by a rise in AFP one month later. The records lack coagulation studies and platelet counts, making it impossible to determine if the renal vein thrombosis was bland or tumour-related. ### 5. Priorities and Next Decisions
The situation is urgent due to the rapid progression of disease and the presence of renal vein thrombosis. The immediate priority is tissue characterisation of the renal mass via biopsy. This is critical because the planned EP chemotherapy is indicated for NSGCT but would be inappropriate for primary RCC. The decision to withhold anticoagulation due to gross haematuria is a necessary safety measure until the nature of the thrombus and the stability of the haematuria are established.
