## Saved final answer

### Clinical Case Evaluation

This retrospective uncontrolled series evaluated high-dose oral vitamin D for chemotherapy- or radiation-associated skin toxicity in 33 patients. Regarding observed improvement, 86.7% (26/30) of patients with available symptom data reported relief within 10 days. Regarding feasibility, 72.7% (24/33) of the total cohort continued anticancer treatment without interruption.

These results suggest that the intervention is feasible within an oncology setting and is associated with symptom improvement. However, the claim that vitamin D caused recovery is unsupported due to the lack of a control group and the fact that 30 patients received concurrent supportive therapy, creating significant confounding.

Claims regarding general safety are limited because the study lacked a systematic safety monitoring protocol and reported no treatment-related adverse events, which may not reflect a comprehensive safety profile. Furthermore, the evidence is weakened by inconsistent laboratory data, with post-treatment vitamin D levels measured in only 13 patients. The absence of patient-level pairing for every outcome and the uncontrolled design prevent the establishment of a causal relationship between the intervention and the reported clinical outcomes. These findings should be viewed as hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory.
