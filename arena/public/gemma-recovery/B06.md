### Clinical Synthesis and Management

A teenage boy presents with fever (39.2°C), tachycardia (103/min), and right-sided forehead and periorbital swelling following two weeks of upper-respiratory symptoms and four days of severe retro-orbital headache. While initial symptoms suggested simple sinusitis, the current presentation of systemic inflammatory signs and localized swelling indicates a progression to a complicated infection.

CT confirms a frontal sinus abscess with a subperiosteal collection. This establishes the presence of a localized abscess but does not establish whether there is marrow involvement or if the infection has extended into the intracranial or orbital spaces, as MRI evidence is absent.

The patient's training history—daily breath-holding dives in a 12-foot pool—is a significant factor. He reported a forehead pop and pain occurring at depth, not during ascent. It is hypothesized that this barotrauma may have predisposed the frontal sinus to subsequent infection, though this remains an unproven link.

Immediate next steps include:
1. Surgical drainage of the frontal sinus abscess and subperiosteal collection to control the source of infection.
2. Initiation of systemic antibiotics to treat the abscess and associated fever.
3. Urgent MRI to evaluate for marrow involvement or occult intracranial/orbital extension not seen on CT.

Justification: The transition from sinusitis to a subperiosteal abscess requires surgical intervention and expanded imaging to rule out life-threatening complications. The diving history provides a potential mechanism for the initial sinus insult, necessitating a focused evaluation of barotrauma-related complications.
