## Case information actually supplied

A prospective uncontrolled series enrolled 15 people with advanced cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma of the head and neck awaiting surgery. All received neoadjuvant capecitabine.
Ten of 15 had clinical tumour reduction. Twelve underwent surgery; five of those twelve had a complete pathological response and seven of twelve were downstaged. Two people with apparent complete clinical lesion resolution chose radiotherapy instead of surgery; another did not proceed to surgery after progression. No grade 3 or higher treatment-related toxicity was reported. There was no comparator group, and follow-up for durability was short.

## Original task

Audience: a clinician. Use 180–240 words in English. Give the clinical-reduction, pathological-complete-response and downstaging percentages with their denominators. Explain why clinical disappearance, pathological response and durable cure are different claims. State what can and cannot be concluded about comparative efficacy and safety. Give brief evidence-based justifications, not a private reasoning trace. Do not invent unreported findings, ages, test results or outcomes. Do not prescribe a dose or claim that an unreported result is normal.

## Saved final answer

### Clinical Evaluation of Neoadjuvant Capecitabine

This prospective uncontrolled series of 15 patients with advanced cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma of the head and neck demonstrated a clinical tumour reduction rate of 66.7% (10/15). Among the 12 patients who proceeded to surgery, the pathological complete response rate was 41.7% (5/12) and the downstaging rate was 58.3% (7/12).

Clinical disappearance refers to the visible reduction or resolution of a lesion, which is a hypothesis of response rather than a confirmation. Pathological response is the factual histological confirmation of tumour eradication in the surgical specimen. Durable cure is a distinct claim regarding long-term survival or recurrence-free intervals, which cannot be inferred here due to theshort follow-up period.

Regarding comparative efficacy and safety, no conclusions can be drawn. The absence of a comparator group precludes any determination of whether capecitabine is superior or inferior to other treatments or surgery alone. Safety cannot be definitively established as the study was uncontrolled and the sample size was small, although no grade 3 or higher treatment-related toxicities were reported in this specific cohort.

The justifications for these distinctions are based on the the fact that clinical imaging or physical examination cannot replace histological verification, and short-term response rates do not equate to long-term oncological outcomes.
