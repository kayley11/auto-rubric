### Clinical Evaluation of IL-6 Receptor Blockade

This retrospective series involves 18 treated attacks across 17 patients with MOGAD or AQP4-positive NMOSD. The cohort is heterogeneous regarding treatment intention: thirteen cases received the blockade as acute escalation following inadequate therapy, while five began it as early maintenance.

The sequence of EDSS scores shows a median improvement from 5.0 at nadir to 3.5 before blockade initiation (P=.001), followed by a further decrease to 2.0 at three months (P=.03). While the data support a correlation between blockade and further disability reduction, the acute rescue effect remains unresolved. The pre-existing improvement (5.0 to 3.5) indicates that recovery was already underway before the drug was administered.

The reported P values indicate statistical significance but do not establish causality. Because there is no concurrent untreated control group, the observed recovery cannot be distinguished from spontaneous recovery or the effects of earlier corticosteroids and plasma exchange. Consequently, the it is unclear whether the IL-6 blockade provided an additive rescue effect or if the patients were simply continuing a natural recovery trajectory. The lack of participant subset data for every paired comparison further limits the definitive interpretation of the clinical benefit.
