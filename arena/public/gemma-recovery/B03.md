### Clinical Synthesis

A woman in her late 70s presents with a 14-month history of severe watery diarrhoea (up to 10x daily, nocturnal) and significant weight loss (26.5 kg). Current findings include hypoalbuminemia (2.3 g/dL), hypokalemia (2.8 mmol/L), hypomagnesemia (0.7 mg/dL), and hypophosphatemia (1.9 mg/dL). The timing of symptom improvement during two hospital admissions—specifically when her usual combination antihypertensive containing olmesartan was not supplied—suggests a potential link between the medication and the gastrointestinal symptoms. However, a short course of budesonide overlapped with these periods, introducing a corticosteroid confounder.

3. **Celiac disease**: Considered but less likely given negative serology.

**Reassessment Plan**
*   **Controlled medication withdrawal**: Deliberately withdraw olmesartan and monitor for resolution of symptoms and weight gain.
*   **Electrolyte replacement**: Immediate correction of potassium, magnesium, and phosphate.
*   **Electrolyte and albumin own-monitoring**: Daily blood tests to monitor recovery.
