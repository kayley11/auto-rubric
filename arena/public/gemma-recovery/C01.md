**Clinical Case Review**

1. **Problem Representation**
An adult with rheumatoid arthritis taking methotrexate and intermittent NSAIDs presents with ankle swelling, morning facial puffiness, and frothy urine, without shortness of breath.

2. **Diagnostic Possibilities**
*   **Nephrotic Syndrome:** Suspected due to the triad of peripheral edema, facial puffiness, and frothy urine (suggestive of significant proteinuria). The patient's rheumatoid arthritis and NSAID use are potential contributing factors.
*   **Drug-Induced Nephrotoxicity:** Suspected given the use of NSAIDs and methotrexate, which can cause interstitial nephritis or glomerular damage leading to edema.
*   **Heart Failure:** Less likely as the patient reports normal breathing, but remains a possibility for bilateral ankle swelling.

3. **Prioritised Investigations**
*   **Urinalysis and Protein/Creatinine Ratio:** To confirm and quantify proteinuria.
*   **Serum Creatinine and Electrolytes:** To assess current glomerular filtration rate and renal function.
*   **Serum Albumin:** To evaluate for hypoalbuminemia consistent with nephrotic-range loss.

4. **Missing Information**
*   **Medical History:** Does the patient have a history of hypertension or diabetes?
*   **Medication Details:** What is the frequency and dose of NSAID use?
*   **Physical Examination:** Is the swelling pitting? Is there evidence of hypertension or joint inflammation?
*   **Symptom Progression:** How rapidly did the facial puffiness and ankle swelling develop?

5. **Safety and Next Steps**
Immediate priority is the assessment of renal function and protein loss to prevent acute kidney injury or severe complications of nephrotic syndrome. If renal impairment is confirmed, a review of NSAID and methotrexate therapy is indicated. The patient should be monitored for signs of pulmonary edema or thromboembolism, which are risks associated with nephrotic syndrome. Further diagnostic steps, such as a renal biopsy, may be necessary if the etiology of the proteinuria remains unclear after initial labs.
