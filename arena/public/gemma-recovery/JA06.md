### Clinical Case Summary

A 30-year-old woman with migraine with aura and oral contraceptive use presented with a five-minute episode of painless left-eye inferior visual field loss. This event differed from her typical aura. Retinal photographs captured during the episode showed a small intravascular retinal embolus with superior macular whitening, which subsequently moved distally as whitening diminished. Symptoms resolved completely, and the post-event neurological examination was normal.

While the rapid resolution and history of migraine might suggest a transient ischemic attack or a migraine-related phenomenon, these do not settle the cause. The documented presence of an intravascular embolus provides objective evidence of a vascular event, distinguishing it from a purely functional aura. However, the source of this embolus remains unconfirmed.

Urgent further assessment is justified to identify a potentially life-threatening embolic source. The main directions of investigation must include brain imaging to rule out intracranial pathology, a comprehensive cardiac evaluation to detect paradoxical emboli or valvular issues, and a shunt study to investigate a patent foramen ovale. Given the patient's profile, identifying the mechanism of the embolus is critical to prevent recurrent strokes or permanent vision loss. These investigations are essential to differentiate between a cardiac, hematologic, or structural vascular etiology.
