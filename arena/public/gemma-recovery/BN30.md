### 1. Problem Representation

Concurrently, the patient exhibits signs of intravascular volume depletion (IVC/Ao ratio 0.4, high urine specific gravity, BUN/Cr ratio 38.3) and profound nephrotic-range proteinuria (UPCR 27.1 g/g) with hypoalbuminemia (2.0 g/dL). The primary uncertainty is the causal link between the massive proteinuria/hypoalbuminemia and the mechanical bowel obstruction.

### 2. Prioritised Explanations
*   **Nephrotic Syndrome causing Bowel Wall Edema:** The massive proteinuria and severe hypoalbuminemia suggest a nephrotic syndrome (e.g., Minimal Change Disease). Low oncotic pressure can lead to significant bowel wall edema, which may result in functional or mechanical SBO. This explains both the urinary findings and the "keyboard sign" (thickened, edematous bowel walls).
*   **Intussusception:** The "to-and-fro" movement and RLQ tenderness are common in intussusception. While the keyboard sign is more typical of generalized edema or obstruction, intussusception remains a primary pediatric cause of SBO. However, this does not explain the nephrotic-range proteinuria.
*   **Acute Appendicitis with Secondary Ileus:** RLQ tenderness and rebound tenderness are classic for appendicitis. This could cause a localized or generalized ileus. However, the degree of proteinuria and hypoalbuminemia is not typical for simple appendicitis.

### 3. Timeline and Evidence Limits
The patient presented with a rapid onset of gastrointestinal symptoms (since the previous evening). The sequence of events suggests a simultaneous presentation of an acute abdomen and nephrotic-range proteinuria. The records do not establish whether the proteinuria preceded the abdominal pain or if both were triggered by a common systemic process. There is no data on the patient's growth history, previous episodes of edema, or response to initial resuscitation.

### 4. Information to Obtain

*   **Serology/Immunology:** Anti-PLA2R or other specific antibodies (via blood test) to narrow the etiology of the proteinuria.
*   **Repeat Imaging after Fluid Resuscitation:** Follow-up ultrasonography to see if the bowel wall thickness decreases as intravascular volume is restored, which would support the edema hypothesis.

### 5. Priorities and Next Decisions

Initial stabilization with fluids and potentially steroids (if nephrotic syndrome is suspected) is indicated before deciding on surgical intervention.
