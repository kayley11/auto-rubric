### Clinical Evaluation: Generalised Oedema

**Case Synthesis**
A woman in her 70s with metastatic NSCLC presents with generalised oedema and hypoalbuminemia (4.6 to 2.1 g/dL) following the initiation of amivantamab. Associated findings include an increase in creatinine (1.1 to 1.83 mg/dL).

**Differential Mechanisms**
1. **Drug-Induced Capillary Leak Syndrome (CLS):** Hypothesised due to the rapid onset of oedema and severe hypoalbuminemia shortly after amivantamab initiation.
2. **Nephrotic Syndrome:** Considered due to oedema and hypoalbuminemia; however, this is weakened by a urine protein:creatinine ratio of 246 mg/g, which is below the nephrotic range.
3. **Organ Failure:** Heart failure and liver dysfunction are excluded by normal echocardiographic function and liver tests. Protein-losing enteropathy is unlikely given the negative faecal alpha-1-antitrypsin.

**Drug-Related Justification**
The temporal relationship between amivantamab administration and the onset of oedema supports a drug-related etiology. The absence of cardiac, hepatic, or significant renal protein loss suggests a systemic redistribution of fluid and protein, consistent with CLS. The rise in creatinine may be a secondary effect of decreased effective circulating volume.

**Clinical Review and Monitoring Plan**
*   **Action:** Monitor daily weights and strict fluid balance to assess volume status.
*   **Action:** Perform serial serum albumin and creatinine measurements to track protein recovery and renal function.
*   **Action:** Conduct regular physical examinations to monitor the extent of abdominal and peripheral oedema.
*   **Justification:** These steps are necessary to quantify the severity of the leak and evaluate the impact of the current treatment regimen on organ perfusion.
