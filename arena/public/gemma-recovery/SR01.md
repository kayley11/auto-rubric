### Clinical Evaluation

**Problem Representation**
A patient presents with a two-year history of progressive spastic paraplegia, T3–T4 sensory level, and urinary incontinence, associated with symmetric long-segment spinal cord lesions and punctate cerebral white-matter abnormalities.

**Prioritised Diagnostic Categories**
1. **Leukodystrophies** (e.g., adult-onset forms): Suggested by the symmetric, ring-like spinal pattern and white-matter distribution.
2. **Neuromyelitis Optica Spectrum Disorder (NMOSD) / MOGAD**: Considered due to long-segment transverse myelitis, though brain imaging is atypical.
3. **Chronic Inflammatory Demyelinating Polyneuropathy or atypical Myelitis**: Less likely given the imaging patterns.

**Distinguishing Investigations**
*   **Genetic testing** (Whole Exome Sequencing) to identify leukodystrophy mutations.
*   **Serum AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG antibodies** to evaluate autoimmune inflammatory etiologies.

**Clinical Justification**
The earlier diagnoses of multiple sclerosis or myelitis are challenged by the CSF findings; the absence of oligoclonal bands and a normal IgG index significantly reduce the likelihood of MS. While the spinal imaging shows long-segment involvement consistent with myelitis, the symmetric ring-like pattern and sparing of the central grey matter are atypical for standard inflammatory myelitis and more suggestive of a metabolic or genetic leukoencephalopathy. However, imaging and CSF alone cannot settle the diagnosis because some inflammatory conditions can present with negative CSF markers, and certain leukodystrophies mimic inflammatory patterns.
