### Clinical Evaluation: Postoperative Thrombocytopenia

**Clinical Synthesis**
The patient presents with a paradoxical haemorrhagic and thrombotic state. Observed facts include severe thrombocytopenia (5×10^9/L), haematuria, and schistocytes on blood film, indicating microangiopathic haemolysis. Simultaneously, the patient developed proximal bilateral deep-vein thromboses with markedly elevated D-dimer (63,355 ng/mL). This combination of profound thrombocytopenia and systemic thrombosis following heparin exposure is highly suggestive of heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT) with thrombosis.

**Interpretation of Discordant Tests**
The anti-PF4 ELISA is strongly positive, confirming the presence of antibodies. The serotonin-release assay (SRA) is inconclusive rather than negative, as strong release occurred independently of heparin. This result does not rule out HIT; the lack of inhibition in the initial experiment leaves the functional significance of the antibodies uncertain. Given the clinical picture, the positive ELISA and inconclusive SRA should be interpreted as supportive of a HIT hypothesis until proven otherwise.

**Priorities for Specialist Review**
Immediate hematological and vascular review is required to:
1. Confirm the diagnosis via repeat or specialised functional assays to resolve the inconclusive SRA.
2. Manage the acute thrombotic burden while balancing the extreme bleeding risk posed by the critical platelet count.
3. Evaluate the contribution of newly prescribed levetiracetam to the hematological abnormalities.
4. Determine the appropriate non-heparin anticoagulant strategy.

Justification: The severity of the thrombocytopenia and the presence of extensive venous thrombosis necessitate urgent specialist intervention to prevent further organ failure or fatal haemorrhage.
