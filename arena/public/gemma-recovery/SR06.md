## Saved final answer

### Treatment Plan for Your Son

Your son has severe mucopolysaccharidosis type II, meaning his body lacks a specific enzyme. Because this enzyme is missing, materials build up in his organs and brain. We are using multiple treatment routes because different methods reach different parts of the body. Intravenous therapy treats the rest of the body, but it cannot easily cross into the brain. Therefore, we added direct delivery into the brain’s fluid spaces to address neurological involvement.

The treatment is staged to ensure safety and effectiveness. We began enzyme replacements first, and we are planning a cord-blood transplant for when he is nine months old. This timing allows for necessary growth and the coordination of a matching donor.

It is important to understand that this plan cannot guarantee a specific developmental outcome. While we are treating multiple areas, we do not yet know how your son will respond or what complications may arise. Broad risks associated with transplants include infection or the body rejecting the donor cells.

When you meet with the specialist team, you may wish to ask:
1. How will we monitor if the brain-directed therapy is working?
2. What are the specific risks of the cord-blood transplant for an infant?
3. How will the team manage potential side effects of the combined treatments?

Justification: Multiple routes are necessary because systemic enzyme replacement does not adequately penetrate the blood-brain barrier in type II disease. Staging allows for patient growth and donor matching. Transplant outcomes remain uncertain at this stage.
