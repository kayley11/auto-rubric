## Final answer, unchanged

**Final Assessment**

This 60-year-old man presents with a subacute progressive cerebellar-brainstem syndrome: continuous ocular flutter, horizontal head tremor, and broad-based gait ataxia over one month, with preserved limb strength, sensation, and reflexes. The mild CSF pleocytosis with negative pathogen mNGS and a structurally normal brain MRI localise the process to a non-structural, likely immune-mediated aetiology.

Prioritised explanations:

1. Autoimmune or paraneoplastic encephalitis targeting the cerebellum or brainstem. The subacute course, ocular flutter, and gait ataxia are characteristic. The negative standard antibody panel reduces the probability of a known-antibody-mediated process but does not exclude it, as the tissue-based assay was not performed and novel antibodies may be present.

2. Occult malignancy with paraneoplastic neurologic syndrome. Tumor markers are normal, but whole-body PET-CT and endoscopy were deferred, so malignancy remains unexcluded.

3. Inflammatory or demyelinating process. MRI argues against classic demyelination, yet oligoclonal bands and IgG index were not assessed, leaving intrathecal immune activation uncharacterised.

Proportionate next steps: perform the tissue-based assay, assess oligoclonal bands and IgG index, complete tumour screening (PET-CT, CT chest/abdomen/pelvis, age-appropriate screening), and consider empiric immunotherapy if clinical suspicion remains high pending results.
