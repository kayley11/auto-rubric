## Final answer, unchanged

**Clinical Problem**

A 24-year-old woman presents with recurrent focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures (1–2/month since age 22), longstanding learning difficulty from approximately age 7, and reported adult cognitive decline (MMSE 12). Structural imaging shows progressive intracranial calcifications—small at age 6, widespread in basal ganglia and cerebellum at 24—plus right thalamic and parietal cysts and diffuse periventricular/deep-white-matter T2/FLAIR hyperintensity without enhancement or diffusion restriction. EEG shows bilateral occipital and parietal sharp and slow waves.

**Provisional Differential**

1. Genetic calcifying encephalopathy (e.g., primary familial brain calcification or related inherited syndrome): most consistent with progressive bilateral calcifications, cystic lesions, white-matter changes, childhood cognitive impairment, seizures, and a brother with mild memory difficulty. Uncertainty: no genetic or metabolic results are available in this packet.
2. Mitochondrial or metabolic encephalopathy: could explain calcifications, white-matter signal change, seizures, and cognitive decline. Uncertainty: no biochemical screening data supplied.
3. Vascular or structural calcification syndrome (e.g., Sturge-Weber spectrum): supported by calcifications and cystic changes, though absence of enhancement and the distribution are less typical.

**Next Assessment**

Serum calcium, phosphate, and parathyroid hormone; formal neuropsychological testing; targeted genetic panel or whole-exome sequencing; and consideration of prolonged EEG monitoring to characterise seizure semiology.
