## Final answer, unchanged

**Clinical Summary**

A 35-year-old woman presents with four days of progressive, painful (9/10) left anterior neck swelling. The mass is soft, markedly tender, and does not move with swallowing. Thyroid function is normal. She reports dysphagia to both solids and liquids, orthopnoea, and breathlessness on mild exertion. Current examination shows no acute airway compromise, but compressive symptoms indicate a risk of deterioration.

**Differential (prioritised)**

1. Deep neck space collection (abscess or phlegmon): supported by softness, tenderness, progressive course, and compressive symptoms. Absence of documented fever is noted but does not exclude this.
2. Soft tissue haematoma: possible given softness and acuity, though no trauma, anticoagulant use, or neck procedure was reported.
3. Subacute thyroiditis: less likely because the mass does not move with swallowing and thyroid function is normal, though early subacute thyroiditis can present with normal TFTs.

**Next actions**

Urgent same-day neck imaging (ultrasound or contrast-enhanced CT) is indicated to characterise the mass and assess for a drainable collection. Baseline bloods (full blood count, CRP, coagulation screen) should be obtained. Given orthopnoea and dysphagia to both solids and liquids, the patient requires close monitoring for airway compromise; a low threshold for escalation to ENT or surgical review is appropriate. Trismus and neck stiffness remain undocumented and should be specifically assessed.
