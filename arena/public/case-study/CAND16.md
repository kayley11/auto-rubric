## Final answer, unchanged

## Clinical Summary

A 58-year-old man presents with a 7-month history of chills, 4-month history of sicca symptoms and joint pain, and 2-month progression to polyarticular swelling involving wrists, finger joints, elbows, shoulders, knees, ankles, and feet, with fatigue and weight loss. Examination reveals pallor, multiple swollen tender joints, and enlarged cervical, axillary, and inguinal lymph nodes. He is afebrile with bradycardia (55/min). Day-0 markers show CRP 205.88 mg/L, ESR 75 mm/h, haemoglobin 77 g/L, and albumin 27.5 g/L. Four months prior, RF and anti-CCP were not elevated, with ESR 54 mm/h and CRP 106.8 mg/L.

## Prioritised Explanations

1. Lymphoproliferative disorder: Multi-site lymphadenopathy, B-type symptoms (chills, weight loss), significant anaemia, hypoalbuminaemia, and markedly elevated CRP collectively raise concern. No imaging or tissue diagnosis has been performed; this remains unconfirmed.

2. Seronegative inflammatory arthritis: The symmetric polyarticular pattern is consistent, but the degree of systemic involvement (lymphadenopathy, anaemia, hypoalbuminaemia) is atypical for isolated arthritis. Negative RF and anti-CCP do not exclude this but reduce its likelihood as the sole explanation.

3. Other systemic inflammatory or infectious process: The bradycardia and multi-system involvement warrant consideration, though no specific evidence currently supports a particular aetiology.

## Next Steps

Before committing to a treatment direction, the following are needed: peripheral blood film, imaging (CT or ultrasound) of lymph node sites, and lymph node or marrow biopsy to exclude lymphoproliferative disease. Additional serology and infection screening should be considered. The negative autoimmune serology and prior rheumatology referral do not establish a rheumatological cause; the systemic features demand a broader diagnostic workup first.
