## Final answer, unchanged

## Clinical Summary

A 35-year-old man with a prior schizophrenia label presents with a three-year progressive decline in social behaviour (loss of empathy, disinhibition, self-neglect, job loss) and a six-month reduction in speech. At referral he was minimally verbal, poorly cooperative, with frontal release signs and brisk reflexes. MMSE was not completed. Multiple antipsychotics and adjuncts showed no meaningful improvement. Family history is negative for early-onset neurodegenerative or psychiatric disease. An initial CT was read as hydrocephalus; its later reinterpretation is not available. Gait is slow with reduced arm swing but no parkinsonian features. Urinary symptoms are unrecorded.

## Differential (prioritised)

1. Frontotemporal dementia, behavioural variant: Progressive behavioural change over three years, frontal release signs, and speech reduction in a 35-year-old are consistent. The prior schizophrenia label and incomplete cognitive assessment prevent confirmation.

2. Normal-pressure hydrocephalus: The initial CT interpretation and reduced arm swing raise this possibility, but gait lacks classic features (no magnetic gait, no festination) and urinary symptoms are unrecorded. The CT wording is not fully specified.

3. Treatment-refractory schizophrenia with functional decline: Cannot be excluded, but the progressive course, frontal signs, and lack of response to multiple agents make this less likely as the sole explanation.

## What cannot be established

The exact onset of the original diagnosis, whether cognitive decline is progressive or static, and whether medication effects or side-effects contributed to the current state, given unverified durations and doses.
