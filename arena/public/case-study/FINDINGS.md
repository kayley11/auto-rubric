# Twenty-two source-based cases: results and findings

**All 22 cases now have saved interviews, answers and rubric results.** Clinical references and candidate information requirements remain **awaiting confirmation**. This is a development study for refining the rubric and its case keys, without independent clinician ratings.

## 1. What was completed

- The first six cases are preserved. The remaining sixteen add 66 question turns, 16 final answers, 105 primary ratings and 10 repeat ratings.
- Together the final cohorts contain **22 answers and 89 question turns**, with **144 primary item ratings** and **33 additional repeat/control ratings**. Repeats and constructed variants are not additional patients.
- arc:lite answered and arc:apex judged using the selected current manuscript anchors. Each case allowed six requests, with source-checked fixed replies. All final answers address clinicians; patient, caregiver and record roles identify the information source.
- The answering model saw the selected opening, task, decision point and actual replies, without article titles, later outcomes, reference answers or the candidate requirement list. There was no new MELD run in this expansion.
- Earlier book cases and N01–N06 remain separate. CAND01–CAND22 refer to the 22 newly selected source reports.

## 3. What the cases reveal

**Information gathering and final judgement need separate evidence.** CAND16 missed candidate exposure/infection requests but synthesised its received evidence well (D5c=2, D3a=5). CAND04 and CAND11 also received D5c=2 but D6a=5. CAND11 explicitly recommended a stroke-code pathway and urgent CT/CTA in its final answer, yet never made those assessment/imaging requests during its two question turns. The author should confirm the required timing and whether an emergency record-review task needs an action channel. A recommendation is not silently credited as an executed request.

**Essential requirements need precise acceptable alternatives.** CAND20 received D5c 5,5,5 when systemic history was helpful; changing only its level to essential produced 2,4,3. The disagreement was whether asking about fever/dyspnoea satisfied the systemic-history requirement without mucosal/skin enquiries. In the additional batch, CAND04 omitted medication history, CAND11 omitted acute assessment/imaging requests and CAND13 omitted current haemodynamic/resuscitation requests under the frozen keys. These examples locate which essential components and alternatives need confirmation.

**Escalation grades need clearer examples for the existing care setting.** The unchanged CAND03 answer received D6a **2,3,4**. It recognised possible adrenal crisis but said only “initiate adequate replacement and monitor response”. The judge alternated between no adequate pathway, a pathway with unclear timing, and a reasonably timely pathway. By comparison, CAND11 received 5,5,5 and CAND13 4,4,4. The earlier CAND18 repeats were 4,3,4. Specify the required additional care/action and timing for patients already in hospital, then calibrate adjacent grades with worked examples.

**Evidence status is a recurring factual distinction.** CAND09 changed an experimentally withheld tissue assay into “not performed” (D1=4). CAND12 changed an unsupplied age-75 amyloid-PET result into “not performed” (D1=3); CAND13 made a similar claim about cross-sectional imaging (D1=3). Not supplied, not measured, pending and negative are different claims. CAND09’s reason describes a minor unsupported error despite selecting the “accurate throughout” grade, giving a concrete D1 3/4-boundary question. The clinical impact still needs judgement.

**Judge explanations can disagree about the same medical claim.** CAND03’s D1=2 explanation criticises the account of stopping osilodrostat and the speculative “non-recoverable axis” statement. Its D5u=5 explanation calls the mechanistic account supported and well calibrated. A clinician should resolve that account and decide any linked effect on representation or uncertainty. Different item scores can be legitimate; opposed factual premises need checking. D3a should not be lowered just because another item is low.

**Source packets need interpretation metadata.** CAND12’s method-context group was deferred and never returned. The answer extended retrospective amyloid-assay status to the whole amyloid/tau panel and to actual clinical availability. Future packets should keep values, sample dates, methods and known result-availability together. Distinguish retrospective research review from what the treating team knew at that time. The saved interview remains unchanged; the written website template includes all selected fact groups and is a separate condition.

The first six also supplied gross-error controls: a fabricated measured creatinine baseline received D1=2,2,2 and an appended definite drug-causation/diabetes-subtype claim received D3d=1,1,1. These are constructed judge checks, not spontaneous model errors. They do not establish reliable handling of subtler grade boundaries.
