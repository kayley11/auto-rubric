## Final answer, unchanged

**Thyroid Profile Review Through 27 January 2023**

This man with oesophageal cancer (pembrolizumab-based therapy from 15 April 2022; oesophagectomy 7 July 2022) developed a biphasic thyroid pattern consistent with immune checkpoint inhibitor thyroiditis: a thyrotoxic phase (TT4 164, TSH <0.004, 6 May 2022) followed by severe primary hypothyroidism (TT4 <3.9, TSH 120, 22 August 2022) on 100 µg levothyroxine. By 27 October, TSH had normalised (2.36) on the same dose. The dose was then increased to 125 µg (rationale undocumented); by 21 November and 27 January 2023, TSH was suppressed (0.06 and 0.08) with TT4 at 64.1 and 45.8 nmol/L respectively (reference 55.34–160.88).

**Prioritised interpretations:**

Over-replacement: The undocumented 25 µg increase preceded TSH suppression. However, the below-reference TT4 in January is atypical for simple excess and may reflect reduced thyroxine-binding globulin post-surgery or weight loss, making total T4 an unreliable marker here.

Resolving ICI thyroiditis with evolving gland function: The initial destructive thyroiditis may have left a partially functioning gland; the interplay between endogenous output and exogenous replacement could produce a suppressed TSH with variable TT4.

Central hypothyroidism (hypophysitis): Less likely given the preceding TSH >100, but ICI-related pituitary dysfunction can develop later. The suppressed TSH with low TT4 would be consistent if the pituitary failed to mount a TSH response.

**Further evidence needed before treatment change:** Free T4 (total T4 is unreliable here), anterior pituitary axis testing (ACTH, morning cortisol, prolactin, IGF-1, gonadotropins), and confirmation of levothyroxine administration conditions. A suppressed TSH alone does not establish over-replacement; the low TT4 warrants investigation before any dose reduction.
