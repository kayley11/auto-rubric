## Final answer, unchanged

**Clinical Problem**

A 57-year-old woman presents with acute kidney injury (creatinine 807 µmol/L on admission, 9 August), progressive anaemia (haemoglobin 64 g/L), haematuria, proteinuria, hypertension, and oedema. Symptoms began approximately ten days prior with diarrhoea and myalgia. A prior creatinine of 456 µmol/L (2 August) shows a 351 µmol/L (77%) rise over seven days. No prior renal disease is documented.

**Prioritised Explanations**

1. Thrombotic microangiopathy (e.g., atypical HUS or complement-mediated TMA): supported by preceding diarrhoea, acute renal failure, anaemia, elevated LDH (352 U/L), C3-positive DAT, and active urinary sediment. Uncertainty: platelets are normal (213 × 10⁹/L), no schistocytes confirmed, no Shiga toxin or complement levels available.

2. Acute glomerulonephritis (post-infectious or ANCA-associated): supported by haematuria (381 RBC/µL), proteinuria (500 mg/dL), hypertension, and rapid creatinine rise. Uncertainty: ANCA, anti-GBM, and complement results are not yet available; cast data absent.

3. Drug-related contribution (ceftazidime, LMWH): both were administered after symptom onset, making them less likely as primary aetiology but possible contributors to renal injury.

**Next Actions**

Obtain peripheral smear (schistocytes), serum C3/C4, ANCA panel, anti-GBM antibody, haptoglobin, reticulocyte count, and stool Shiga toxin. Urine microscopy for casts is pending.
