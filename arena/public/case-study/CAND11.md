**Emergency Assessment Pre-Imaging**

**Clinical problem.** A 61-year-old woman developed sudden altered mental status, slurred speech, and gait instability approximately one hour after a three-port pars plana vitrectomy performed under local anaesthesia. The operative record documents a suspected expulsive suprachoroidal haemorrhage (dome-shaped choroidal elevation, estimated 2 mL, posterior segment only) occurring while the team was preparing for silicone-oil tamponade. No intraocular gas type or concentration is recorded. No direct observation of air entering the circulation is documented. No intraoperative hypotension or Valsalva manoeuvre is noted. First-minutes post-deterioration interventions (positioning, oxygen) are not documented in the available record.

**Prioritised provisional differential.**
1. Cerebral air embolism – suspected mechanism, not confirmed. Temporal association with vitrectomy and the absence of documented gas tamponade leave the possibility open, but no direct evidence of air entry is recorded.
2. Acute ischaemic stroke – embolic or thrombotic. Temporal proximity to surgery; no specific vascular risk factors documented in this packet.
3. Expulsive suprachoroidal haemorrhage with secondary haemodynamic compromise – observed finding, but 2 mL confined to the posterior segment is unlikely to produce acute neurological deterioration alone.

**Immediate actions.** Activate stroke-code pathway and obtain urgent CT head with CTA. Secure airway, administer supplemental oxygen, continuous monitoring. Perform a focused neurological examination including NIHSS. Retrieve the full anaesthetic record to clarify any undocumented intraoperative events. Escalate to neurosurgery and ophthalmology simultaneously.
