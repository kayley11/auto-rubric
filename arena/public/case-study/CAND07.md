## Final answer, unchanged

**Clinical Problem**

An 80-year-old woman with non-Hodgkin lymphoma (prior rituximab-based therapy; subtype unstated) and HFpEF presented with syncope, hypotension (86/58 mmHg), and bilateral pitting oedema. Admission studies revealed nephrotic-range proteinuria (UPCR 10 g/g), creatinine 1.8 mg/dL, and hypoalbuminaemia (3.0 g/dL). An IgM lambda monoclonal gammopathy was identified with a markedly elevated lambda free light chain (131 mg/L; ratio 0.11). Adrenal insufficiency was diagnosed and treated empirically; no confirmatory cortisol or ACTH values are on file.

**Prioritised Explanations**

1. Monoclonal-gammopathy-associated renal disease (e.g., AL amyloidosis, monoclonal immunoglobulin deposition disease): supported by the IgM lambda clone, heavy proteinuria, and low albumin. Uncertainty: no tissue diagnosis yet; renal biopsy is scheduled.
2. Adrenal insufficiency as a contributor to hypotension and syncope: treated with hydrocortisone and fludrocortisone, but confirmatory biochemistry is absent, so the degree to which it explains the haemodynamic picture remains uncertain.
3. Cardiac decompensation (HFpEF): oedema and hypotension are consistent, yet echocardiography showed preserved EF and no structural abnormality; BNP is not supplied.

**Proportionate Next Steps**

Proceed with the scheduled renal biopsy for tissue diagnosis. Confirm adrenal insufficiency with a morning cortisol and ACTH if not previously measured. Repeat renal function and protein quantification at the four-week follow-up. Clarify the positive HCV antibody (RNA negative) to exclude a false positive or resolved infection. Consider fat-pad biopsy or serum/urine electrophoresis to further characterise the monoclonal protein.
