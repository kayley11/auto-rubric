A late-teenage female with a five-year history of emotionally and physically triggered syncope, escalating over six months, presents with a monitored pre-hospital sequence of Mobitz II block progressing to complete heart block with a narrow-QRS escape (40 bpm) and ventricular standstill >20 s with apnoea, requiring transcutaneous pacing. Inpatient telemetry over seven days captured symptomatic intermittent 2:1 AV block and P-wave asystole up to 9 s despite no rate-limiting drugs. Structural heart disease, ischaemia, and metabolic causes are excluded (normal echo, troponin, biochemistry, chest radiograph). Baseline ECG shows sinus bradycardia (45 bpm) with first-degree AV block. Working diagnosis: profound cardioinhibitory vasovagal syncope refractory to conservative measures and midodrine. The unresolved uncertainties are the absence of tilt-table confirmation, the young age placing her outside pacing guideline criteria (≥40 years), and the possibility of an intrinsic conduction system abnormality mimicking neurally mediated syncope.

## 2. Prioritised Explanations
1. **Cardioinhibitory vasovagal syncope (mixed/vasodepressor features)** — supported by trigger-related episodes (emotional distress, injury), pallor preceding collapse, and documented asystolic pauses. Conflicting: episodes with AV dissociation occurred without identifiable triggers, and pauses were profound and recurrent, which is atypical for benign vasovagal syncope.
2. **Intrinsic conduction system disease (e.g., progressive AV block)** — supported by resting first-degree AV block, sinus bradycardia, Mobitz II progression, and a narrow-QRS junctional escape, suggesting a His–Purkinje-level process. Conflicting: normal structural heart and no family history of conduction disease or sudden death.
3. **Situational syncope / POTS** — considered and reasonably deprioritised given multiple spontaneous AV dissociation episodes without triggers; POTS is inconsistent with documented bradycardia/asystole.

## 3. Timeline and Evidence Limits
Events progressed from chronic triggered syncope → six-month escalation → pre-hospital monitored complete heart block with standstill → stabilisation in sinus bradycardia → seven-day telemetry showing recurrent 2:1 block and asystole → failed conservative measures and midodrine → EP referral. Limits: no tilt-table test (the accepted confirmatory tool) was performed; no Holter/implantable loop recorder data before or after admission; no formal electrophysiology study; the specific conservative measures and their duration are undocumented; multidisciplinary discussion content is unavailable. The record cannot establish whether pauses were exclusively neurally mediated or partly intrinsic, nor the natural history off therapy.

## 4. Information to Obtain
- **Head-up tilt table test with continuous ECG**: would confirm cardioinhibitory physiology and characterise the vasodepressor component, directly informing whether pacing (with or without closed-loop stimulation) would address the mechanism.
- **Formal electrophysiology study (His bundle intervals)**: to exclude intrinsic AV nodal/His–Purkinje disease, which would change the diagnosis and strengthen the indication for pacing regardless of age.
- **Prolonged ambulatory monitoring (implantable loop recorder)**: to quantify pause frequency, circadian pattern, and correlate with symptoms if any doubt remains before committing to a permanent device.
- **Detailed documentation of conservative trial and MDT/EP consultation**: from clinical notes, to verify adequacy of non-device therapy and the individualised rationale for pacing outside guideline age criteria.
- **Medication review including mirtazapine**: to formally exclude pharmacological bradycardic contribution.

## 5. Priorities and Next Decisions
The documented decision to pursue definitive therapy is defensible: recurrent monitored asystole >9 s with apnoea and standstill >20 s constitutes a high-risk, potentially life-threatening bradyarrhythmia unresponsive to first-line measures. Pacing guidelines restricting permanent pacing to ≥40 years create genuine tension, but the severity here justifies individualised, MDT-endorsed escalation. Before device selection, tilt testing and/or EP study should be obtained to clarify mechanism; if intrinsic conduction disease is found, standard dual-chamber pacing is indicated. If vasovagal physiology is confirmed, a dual-chamber system with closed-loop stimulation is the evidence-based choice for this age group. Thorough counselling regarding lifetime device burden, generator changes, and lead management in a young patient is essential before implantation.
