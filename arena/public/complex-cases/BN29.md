Case input

A male in his late 50s has had recurrent lower urinary tract symptoms over approximately 6 years, initially managed as benign prostatic hyperplasia and later as tuberculous cystitis. Despite multiple courses of medical therapy and limited surgical approaches, symptoms persisted, and repeat imaging and cystoscopy showed progressive bladder wall thickening extending to the prostate and distal ureters. He was readmitted for further evaluation after failure of conservative and medical management.

Approximately five years before the final admission, he had persistent lower urinary tract symptoms without systemic symptoms. Ultrasound showed prostatic enlargement with normal prostate-specific antigen (PSA) levels. Cystoscopy showed an enlarged, high-walled prostate with grossly normal mucosa, and transurethral resection of the prostate (TURP) was performed. Histopathology demonstrated benign prostatic hyperplasia with chronic prostatitis.

Approximately four years before the final admission, he presented with gross haematuria. Repeat cystoscopy and TURP revealed petechial haemorrhages along the bladder wall and fibrotic changes in the prostate. Histopathology showed non-caseating granulomas with Langhans and foreign body-type giant cells.

Approximately three years before the final admission, cystoscopy revealed areas of bladder wall thickening and transurethral resection of the bladder (TURBT) was performed. Histopathology revealed xanthogranulomatous cystitis, including areas of caseating and non-caseating granulomas with Langhans and foreign body type multinucleated giant cells. HIV screening was negative. Urine culture and urine TB RT-PCR were negative; however, the attending specialist empirically treated him for extra-pulmonary tuberculous cystitis, and he completed standard anti-tuberculosis therapy (2HRZE/4HR).

Within 2 years before the final admission, dysuria and pyuria persisted. Surveillance cystoscopy showed progressive bladder wall thickening with yellowish bases and petechial changes on the prostate. Antibiotic therapy yielded only minimal relief. Three months before the final admission, MRI of the lumbosacral spine was unremarkable except for left hydroureteric changes. Urine studies revealed sterile pyuria, prompting admission for definitive management.

At the current reassessment, the documented working diagnosis was xanthogranulomatous cystoprostatitis with a possible tuberculous component, given prior histologic and clinical findings. Differential diagnoses, although not strongly considered, included urothelial carcinoma of the bladder or prostate, primary genitourinary tuberculosis and chronic bacterial prostatitis. This working diagnosis remains provisional.

On the current admission, cystoscopy revealed diffuse yellowish thickening across the bladder and a markedly trabeculated, non-distensible bladder with a capacity of 40–50 mL, with similar involvement of the prostate. Given significant tissue and function loss of the bladder, radical cystoprostatectomy with ileal conduit was offered to the patient, to which the patient agreed.

Recorded evidence limits: the source reports negative urine culture and urine TB RT-PCR at the time of the third procedure, and sterile pyuria on later urine studies, but does not report repeated mycobacterial cultures, interferon-gamma release assays, acid-fast staining, or upper-tract imaging beyond the noted left hydroureteric change on lumbosacral MRI. Malignancy exclusion rests on prior resection histopathology; no dedicated staging imaging or repeat biopsy at the current admission is reported in the supplied record. Unreported data are unknown, not normal.

This is a constructed selected-record review: the findings above were acquired at different times over approximately six years, and the record does not provide a single uniform pretreatment time point. The assessment point is the current reassessment before any radical surgery; later radical specimen findings, final extent of inflammation, postoperative function and symptom outcome are withheld and must not be assumed.

**Xanthogranulomatous cystoprostatitis (inflammatory, non-malignant).** Supported by repeated granulomatous histology with xanthomatous change, negative urine studies, and progressive fibrotic bladder contracture. **Genitourinary tuberculosis.** Supported by sterile pyuria, caseating granulomas, and hydroureteric change. Conflicting: negative urine TB RT-PCR and culture, negative HIV screen, and no clinical response to a full 2HRZE/4HR course. Extrapulmonary TB can be paucibacillary, so negative microbiology does not fully exclude it, but the treatment failure substantially weakens this.

**Occult urothelial carcinoma (bladder or prostatic urethra/ducts).** Not strongly considered in the record, but must be excluded before radical surgery. Prior resection histology showed no malignancy; however, no repeat biopsy or staging imaging at the current admission is documented, and carcinoma can coexist with or mimic chronic granulomatous cystitis.

The course progressed from BPH-like symptoms (TURP, benign histology), to haematuria with granulomas, to xanthogranulomatous cystitis, then to a contracted bladder with upper-tract obstruction — a pattern of unrelenting inflammatory destruction despite medical therapy. Limits: mycobacterial cultures, AFB staining, and IGRA were never reported; upper-tract imaging rests only on an incidental finding on lumbosacral MRI; and malignancy exclusion relies on histology from earlier resections, not current tissue. Unreported data are unknown, not normal.
