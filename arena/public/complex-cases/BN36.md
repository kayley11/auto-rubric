Case input

History and exposure context
A man in his 50s from North India has CLL diagnosed six months earlier and is taking acalabrutinib 100 mg twice daily. For two months he has had mildly pruritic, erythematous, scaly plaques on the trunk and limbs. He did not report sensory or motor deficits during the history.

Objective examination
There are multiple well-defined infiltrated dusky erythematous scaly plaques, from 0.5 × 0.5 cm to 8 × 5 cm, over the neck, trunk and limbs. Examination finds approximately 50% sensory loss over plaques, infiltrated earlobes and glove-pattern sensory loss in both hands without motor weakness. Both ulnar and common peroneal nerves are thickened, firm and non-tender. There is no lymphadenopathy or hepatosplenomegaly. Nerve-conduction testing reports sensory neuropathy in both upper limbs.

Available laboratory and tissue findings

Slit-skin smears from two back plaques and both earlobes are negative.

The duration or progression of the objectively detected sensory deficit is not established by the patient’s initial denial. A detailed exposure/contact history, dated baseline neurological map, additional microbiological assessment and a complete drug-interaction/bleeding assessment are not supplied. No final diagnostic label, selected treatment, oncological clearance or later nerve/skin response is provided.
