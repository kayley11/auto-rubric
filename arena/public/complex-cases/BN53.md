A man in his 60s presents with a six-month, painless, ~15 cm left-upper-quadrant cystic mass. CT shows a thin-walled, possibly septated 13.6 × 9.5 × 15.4 cm cyst of uncertain organ of origin with a normal pancreas and reported obstructive uropathy of unquantified severity. EUS describes a thick-walled, multiseptate cyst; aspirate was bloody with negative string test, unremarkable amylase/CEA, and non-diagnostic cytology showing only isolated atypical cells. No tissue lineage, final diagnosis, or renal functional data is established. The key unresolved question is the nature and origin of the cyst — neoplastic (cystic tumour) versus non-neoplastic (pseudocyst, lymphangioma, mesenteric/retroperitoneal cyst) — and whether the atypical cells warrant exclusion of malignancy.

## 2. Prioritised explanations
**Cystic neoplasm (pancreatic or extra-pancreatic, e.g., cystadenocarcinoma, lymphangioma, malignant degeneration):** Supported by large size, septations, thick wall on EUS, bloody aspirate, and isolated atypical cells. Conflicting: low CEA and amylase argue against mucinous and pseudocyst respectively; normal pancreas and absent duct dilatation make a primary pancreatic cyst less secure.

**Pancreatic pseudocyst:** Considered radiologically, but conflicting evidence dominates — no pancreatitis history, normal pancreatic parenchyma, negative string test, unremarkable amylase, and bloody (rather than turbid) aspirate. Unlikely, though a walled-off collection from an unrecognised remote insult cannot be fully excluded.

**Non-pancreatic cystic lesion (splenic/renal/mesenteric/retroperitoneal cyst, lymphangioma):** The uncertain organ of origin and left-sided location make this plausible; the bland fluid and macrophages are compatible, but the atypical cells and septations keep a neoplasm in the differential.

## 3. Timeline and evidence limits
The mass was noted six months before imaging. CT preceded EUS; the differing wall descriptions (thin on CT, thick on EUS) reflect different modalities, not demonstrated progression. EUS-FNA followed a judgement that hydatid disease was unlikely, but the supporting exposure assessment is undocumented. Records cannot establish: temporal change, organ of origin, degree and laterality of obstruction and renal function trend, numerical amylase/CEA, full cytological detail, or a complete risk–benefit discussion of options (MRI, surgery) that were declined for financial reasons.

## 4. Information to obtain
- **Original CT/MRI review or new MRI:** to define organ of origin, wall/septal enhancement, and nodularity — this would substantially narrow the differential and inform resectability.
- **Quantitative renal assessment (creatinine, split renal function, ultrasound of collecting system):** to grade the obstructive uropathy and urgency of decompression.
- **Formal cytology review / repeat targeted biopsy of wall or septa, ideally with cell-block and immunostains:** to characterise the atypical cells and establish lineage; this directly determines whether malignancy must be excluded before conservative management.
- **Documented hydatid serology or exposure history:** to justify the pre-FNA decision retrospectively.
- **Tumour markers (CA 19-9, CEA serum) and documented MCV/iron studies:** to support neoplastic assessment and explain the mild microcytic anaemia.

## 5. Priorities and next decisions
The recorded decisions — choosing EUS alone for financial reasons and proceeding to FNA — were reasonable first steps, but the outcome is non-diagnostic with atypical cells, which is an unsafe endpoint. Priority is exclusion of a cystic malignancy given size, septations, and cytological atypia, alongside assessment of obstructive uropathy, which adds urgency. Surgical exploration with resection and definitive histology is the most defensible next step if malignancy cannot be excluded non-invasively; pre-operative characterisation (MRI, renal function, cytology review) should precede it. Conservative follow-up is not justified while atypical cells remain unexplained.
