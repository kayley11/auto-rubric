Case input

Background and acute course
An adolescent male with established Evans syndrome presented with two weeks of headache, nausea and vomiting, worse at night and in the morning. He reported intermittent left-hand numbness/weakness and diplopia. Initial MRI showed a right frontal white-matter lesion about 4.0 × 3.0 × 3.3 cm, oedema, right lateral-ventricular mass effect and about 7 mm right-to-left midline shift. Three smaller lesions were in the left frontal lobe, corpus callosum and right lateral-ventricular trigone. Enhancement was mild and the dominant lesion had a rim of restricted diffusion.

CSF and initial assessment
CSF contained 61 nucleated cells/µL, 98% lymphocytes, with normal protein and glucose. IgG index and synthesis rate were normal, but supernumerary oligoclonal bands were reported. The exact band count and paired-serum pattern are unavailable; CSF restriction cannot be independently confirmed from this summary. MOG-IgG and AQP4-IgG were negative. The documented evaluation for infectious, rheumatological, lymphoproliferative and neoplastic alternatives was described as negative, but its component methods/results are not supplied.

He received intravenous dexamethasone before lumbar puncture, a single IVIG dose of unspecified indication and a documented course of intravenous methylprednisolone. Headache resolved and neurological function improved during admission. Plasma exchange was not documented. Exact calendar dates and age are withheld in the source.

Subsequent neurological record
Follow-up MRI described extensive bilateral cerebral T2/FLAIR hyperintense white-matter abnormalities with T1 hypointensity and no abnormal restricted diffusion. A right frontoparietal enhancing focus measured about 13 × 11 mm and was smaller than on its preceding scan; left periventricular enhancing foci of about 9 mm and 5 mm were also smaller. Some left frontal enhancement resolved. No new lesions or significant hemispheric atrophy were reported; mild posterior callosal atrophy was unchanged. These descriptions do not establish that every later focus is identical to the original dominant lesion. At the selected clinical review, he denied recurrent neurological symptoms; family described stable school performance and examination was stable.

Immune findings and limits
During rituximab exposure, circulating B cells were absent and IgG/IgM reduced. Naive CD4/CD8 proportions and stimulated CD40-ligand/ICOS expression were reduced; direct antiglobulin testing was positive and soluble IL-2 receptor mildly raised. SAP expression and XIAP testing were essentially normal, but a CD19 component could not be reported. Chromosome-breakage/telomere studies were normal; a primary-immunodeficiency panel and whole-exome sequencing did not identify a unifying pathogenic finding. Pretreatment immunoglobulins, exact quantitative trends, timing of all immune tests, detailed infection history and a current complete safety assessment are not supplied. No final diagnostic synthesis, selected maintenance regimen, long-term immunoglobulin replacement plan or later outcome is provided.
