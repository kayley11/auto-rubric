# BN35 — Progressive food intolerance and weight loss after an initially normal-looking endoscopy


Task

Audience: a clinician. Use 350–500 words in English, including headings. Assess the supplied records at the stated decision point. Give brief clinical justifications, not a private reasoning trace.
Use these five sections:
1. Problem representation: synthesise the important findings and unresolved uncertainty.
2. Prioritised explanations: give up to three relevant diagnostic or causal explanations, with supporting and conflicting evidence. Do not force three if fewer are defensible.
3. Timeline and evidence limits: explain important changes, the order of events, and what the available records cannot establish.
4. Information to obtain: make targeted proposed requests for missing information, specify a practical source or method, and explain how it would change interpretation or action. Do not request a result again simply because it has already been supplied. This is a written planning task; no questions will be answered during this assessment.
5. Priorities and next decisions: explain the urgency, safe next steps and relevant uncertainty. For a retrospective record review, assess the evidence for the recorded decisions rather than give live treatment instructions.
Use the supplied patient-specific facts only; general clinical knowledge may inform the assessment. Keep observations, hypotheses and proposals distinct. Unreported information is unknown, not normal. Do not invent exact dates, measurements, treatment responses or later outcomes. Do not provide rubric scores.


Case input

Previous disease and investigation
A woman in her 40s had biopsy-proven sarcoidosis with cardiac and pulmonary involvement three years before this admission. The original FDG-PET also showed gastric mucosal uptake, but that finding was not pursued at the time. She improved on methotrexate and glucocorticoids, which were stopped after radiological remission.

Current course
Over approximately 12 months she lost 21 kg, reported as 34.4% of baseline weight; 11 kg of the loss occurred during the last six months. She has marked sarcopenia, weakness, secondary amenorrhoea and iron-deficiency anaemia. Distressing food-related odours, nausea, vomiting and abdominal discomfort prevent eating despite preserved appetite and hunger.

An earlier gastroscopy, when the symptoms were initially interpreted as dysphagia, described normal-looking oesophageal, gastric and duodenal mucosa. Samples were taken only from the oesophagus and were unremarkable. No gastric tissue result from that examination is supplied.

Examination and further work-up
She has hyperpigmented macules and a pepper-and-salt rash described as compatible with cutaneous sarcoidosis. A comprehensive immunological panel was unremarkable. ACE reached 62 U/L (reference 12–55). A Synacthen test was reported as excluding adrenal insufficiency. Nasal endoscopy was unremarkable. Brain MRI showed no abnormal enhancement; EEG and lumbar puncture were reported as normal, with no focal neurological deficit. Whole-body CT did not identify active systemic sarcoidosis or another explanation. Psychiatric assessment did not support an eating disorder, although a first-degree relative had anorexia nervosa.

Nutrition and missing detail
Parenteral nutrition was started because of severe malnutrition. A nasogastric feeding attempt worsened symptoms and enteral-access options were being considered. Current electrolyte concentrations, phosphate, magnesium, thiamine status, recent intake, fluid balance, feeding rate and the complete nutritional monitoring record are not supplied. There is no repeat endoscopy, gastric biopsy, confirmed current gastric diagnosis or subsequent treatment response in this packet.
