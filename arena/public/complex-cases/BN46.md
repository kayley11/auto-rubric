A late-20s woman, gravida I para I, now ~5 months post-caesarean delivery, presenting with her third episode of delayed, recurrent, heavy vaginal bleeding — this time with haemodynamic instability and decreased consciousness. The central unresolved question is the source and mechanism of the bleeding — in particular whether an abnormal vessel (e.g., pseudoaneurysm or arteriovenous malformation) at the caesarean isthmic scar is responsible, which cannot be established from the available B-mode ultrasound descriptions.

## 2. Prioritised explanations
1. **Uterine scar-related vascular lesion (pseudoaneurysm or AVM) at the isthmocele site** — most consistent with the pattern: delayed, recurrent, profuse bleeding after caesarean; lesion fixed at the isthmus across both scans; failure of uterotonics (implying bleeding is not from the atonic myometrial bed); haemodynamic instability. Conflicting: no Doppler was documented, so this remains unconfirmed; the day-26 "thrombus" reading may have been a misinterpreted vascular structure.
2. **Isthmocele with accumulated blood and episodic rupture/evacuation** — supported by the day-156 evacuation of a large clot volume with temporary cessation. Conflicting: this mechanism alone does not well explain instability and rapid re-bleeding after evacuation.
3. **Retained products of conception / endometritis** — recorded considerations; both are made unlikely by the empty cavity, negative β-hCG, and absence of fever or pelvic signs, though chronic endometritis cannot be fully excluded without histology.

## 3. Timeline and evidence limits
Day 26: first bleed; isthmic lesion called "thrombus"; resolved medically. Day 156: second bleed; large isthmocele with blood; cervical evacuation gave temporary control. Day 157: catastrophic re-bleed with instability within 24 hours of evacuation. The record cannot establish: whether Doppler interrogation was performed at any point; the current haemoglobin and coagulation status; whether bleeding is active arterial; the exact anatomic source; and whether the day-26 and day-156 lesions are the same structure. The escalating severity over 5 months suggests a lesion that has enlarged or become less stable.

## 4. Information to obtain
- **Doppler ultrasound of the isthmic lesion** (transvaginal colour/spectral Doppler): would directly test for pseudoaneurysm (swirl, high-velocity to-and-fro waveform) or AVM; a positive result mandates angiography and embolisation planning and contraindicates blind curettage.

- **Gynaecology/interventional radiology consultation and pelvic angiography availability**: defines whether uterine artery embolisation is feasible if bleeding continues.
- **Prior operative note of the caesarean**: may reveal difficulties with closure of the uterine incision.
