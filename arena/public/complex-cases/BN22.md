Detailed family history identified no stroke, cerebrovascular disease, arterial dissection, aneurysmal disease or known hereditary connective tissue disorder among first-degree relatives, and no personal history of childhood central nervous system infection or cranial radiotherapy. He reported chickenpox in childhood but no recent zoster rash or herpetic lesions. Neurological and systemic examination was unremarkable, with no phenotypic features suggestive of an inherited connective tissue disorder. In the absence of a suggestive phenotype or relevant family history, targeted genetic testing was not pursued.

Non-contrast CT of the brain on presentation was unremarkable. CT angiography of the head and neck performed the following day demonstrated narrowing of the distal M1 segment of the right middle cerebral artery. Gadolinium-enhanced sequences demonstrated an enhancing lesion in the right basal ganglia, favoured to represent a subacute infarct. Magnetic resonance angiography demonstrated signal abnormality in the right M1 segment; however, the underlying vessel wall pathology remained difficult to characterise on standard sequences, and the appearances could not reliably distinguish severe stenosis from early occlusion or identify the underlying aetiology.

A comprehensive investigation panel was performed during the inpatient admission and is summarised in the article's table 1 (the table contents are not reproduced in this record). An electroencephalogram was performed 10 days after presentation because of the recurrent stereotyped nature of the episodes; this was unremarkable, making an epileptic mimic less likely in the clinical context.

The case was discussed at a neuroradiology multidisciplinary meeting.
