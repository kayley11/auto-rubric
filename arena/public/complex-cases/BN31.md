# Clinical Assessment: Postoperative Chylomediastinum, POD 8

## 1. Problem Representation
A woman in her early 40s with significant comorbidity (BMI 45, diabetes, tobacco use) developed confirmed chylomediastinum after subaortic membrane resection via median sternotomy. Drainage turned milky on POD 2 after oral intake, with triglyceride 1095 mg/dL. Conservative management (NPO, TPN, water-seal drainage) failed to reduce output (~400–500 mL/day). Intranodal lymphangiography on POD 8 showed contrast extravasation and pooling in the superior mediastinal lymphatic chain without a discrete thoracic duct injury; cannulation and embolisation attempts were unsuccessful. The unresolved problem is persistent high-output chylous leakage with no identifiable embolisation target and no response to first-line conservative measures.

## 2. Prioritised Explanations
1. **Injury to mediastinal lymphatic vessels/thoracic duct tributaries during dissection** — strongly supported by the timing (after oral intake), markedly elevated triglyceride, and lymphangiographic extravasation along the superior mediastinal chain. Excessive mediastinal adipose tissue and suspected thymic hyperplasia increased the risk of lymphatic injury during electrocautery dissection.
2. **Discrete thoracic duct injury** — considered but not confirmed; lymphangiography did not demonstrate a main duct injury, and cannulation failed, so this remains neither proven nor excluded.
3. **Alternative causes of milky drainage** (infection/pus, pseudochylous fluid, residual surgical material) — reasonably considered and judged unlikely given no pleural entry, no reported signs of infection, and the biochemical and lymphangiographic findings. These are adequately excluded by the available data.
