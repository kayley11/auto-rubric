Background and course
A woman in her 40s has common variable immunodeficiency diagnosed five years earlier, granulomatous-lymphocytic interstitial lung disease, immune-mediated thrombocytopenia, splenomegaly and generalised lymphadenopathy. She receives weekly intravenous immunoglobulin and prophylactic azithromycin three times weekly. Several weeks of painless bilateral blurred vision progressed over days. No specific occupational or home toxic/infective exposure was identified.

Eye and neurological findings
Visual acuity is 3/60 in each eye; an examination four years previously recorded 6/9 in the right and 6/12 in the left. There are large central scotomas, markedly impaired colour vision, a left relative afferent pupillary defect, sluggish light responses and light–near dissociation. Intraocular pressures are normal and the anterior segments are quiet. Both optic discs are swollen, with grade 1+ vitreous cells. OCT confirms disc swelling with mildly reduced macular ganglion-cell thickness. There is no macular oedema or retinal vasculitis. No other neurological findings are reported.

Laboratory evidence

Culture, molecular and antigen-based tests for the reported viral, tuberculosis, toxoplasma and bartonella investigations are negative. Imaging, CSF and electrophysiology

Full-field ERG is normal. Pattern ERG has reduced P50 amplitudes and an early P50 peak; N95 cannot be assessed reliably because of movement-related baseline drift. Pattern visual evoked potentials are present but poorly formed, preventing reliable quantification; flash responses are simplified.

Timing limits
The publication groups initial and repeat MRI findings three months apart. This packet supplies the selected diagnostic findings together; it does not establish the exact order of all tests, their timing relative to IVIg infusions, or which results preceded initial treatment. Interim treatment exposure and response are not provided.
