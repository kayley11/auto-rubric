A woman in her 70s presents with nephritic–nephrotic overlap features: dyspnoea, severe oedema, hypertension (up to 207/81 mmHg), impaired renal function (creatinine 158 µmol/L, urea 23 mmol/L), hypoalbuminaemia (30 g/L), heavy proteinuria (ACR 232.3 mg/mmol; PCR 332 mg/mmol) and dysmorphic haematuria. Serology shows a high-titre ANA (1:640, homogeneous) without anti-dsDNA, negative ANCA, anti-GBM and normal complement. Biopsy shows a membranoproliferative pattern (mesangial hypercellularity, double contours, interposition) with mesangial and subendothelial fibrillar deposits averaging 7.3 nm width, and full-house immunostaining (C3, C1q, IgG, IgM, C4, IgA). DNAJB9, amyloid A and Congo red are negative. The key unresolved question is the nature of the fibrillary deposits and whether an underlying systemic or clonal process drives the glomerulopathy.

## 2. Prioritised Explanations

**Fibrillary glomerulonephritis (FGN):** The fibril diameter (~7.3 nm, within the 12–30 nm range for typical FGN but reported cases exist with thinner fibrils), mesangial and subendothelial distribution, and oligoclonal IgG kappa band support this. However, DNAJB9 negativity argues against conventional FGN, and full-house staining with C1q is atypical (FGN usually shows IgG-dominant, C3-limited staining).

**Immune-complex membranoproliferative GN with an autoimmune or clonal driver:** High-titre ANA, full-house immunostaining and C1q deposition suggest immune-complex disease; the small IgG kappa band and oligoclonal pattern raise a possible monoclonal gammopathy-associated GN. Conflicting: anti-dsDNA negative, complement normal, no cryoglobulins, and the fibrillar ultrastructure is not explained by standard immune-complex disease.

**Complement-dysregulation-related MPGN (C3 glomerulopathy spectrum):** Supported by strong C3 staining and double contours; but C3 nephritic factor and CFHR3/1/CFB genetics are negative, C4 and C1q are positive (arguing against isolated alternative-pathway activation), and fibrils are atypical.

## 3. Timeline and Evidence Limits

Events: initial assessment with serology, infection/clonal screening and imaging → renal biopsy with immunohistochemistry, electron microscopy and fibril measurement. Complement-dysregulation testing is documented "around this evaluation" but exact sampling dates relative to biopsy are not stated, so temporal relationships are uncertain. The record cannot establish: whether the ANA or paraprotein preceded or followed renal disease, whether the IgG kappa band is transient or clonal, whether complement was consumed earlier, or any pre-existing renal baseline. Later protein studies, neuromuscular events and outcomes are withheld and unknown.

## 4. Information to Obtain

- **Serum free light chains and repeat SPEP/immunofixation** (haematology laboratory): to characterise the IgG kappa band and exclude a clonal B-cell process driving the deposits; a rising clone would prompt haematology referral.
- **Extended autoantibody panel** (rheumatology serology): anti-C1q, anti-histone, ENA subset, and rheumatoid factor/cryoglobulin re-check, to clarify whether the ANA reflects latent autoimmunity relevant to full-house staining.
- **Biopsy review for DNAJB9 technique confirmation and fibril typing** (renal pathology): confirm immunoperoxidase was adequate; consider immunoelectron microscopy for IgG subclass/kappa-lambda restriction within deposits, which would distinguish monoclonal-deposit disease from polyclonal FGN.
- **Prior renal function and urinalysis records** (primary care/previous admissions): to date disease onset and rate of decline.

## 5. Priorities and Next Decisions

The evidence supports a fibrillary/immune-complex MPGN with uncertain classification; the recorded workup was appropriately broad. Urgency is moderate–high given hypertension and renal impairment: blood pressure control and oedema management are priorities, with monitoring of renal function and proteinuria. The main decision point is whether to pursue haematological assessment for the monoclonal band before immunosuppression, since monoclonal-associated deposits may require clone-directed therapy rather than empiric immunosuppression.
