## 2. Prioritised Explanations
1. **PTLD (monomorphic B-cell type likely):** Strongly supported by the monoclonal B-cell population, splenomegaly, unexplained fever, progressive anaemia, and graft dysfunction unresponsive to rejection therapy. Risk factors include prior EBV-positive donor/EBV-seropositive recipient mismatch context, intense immunosuppression history, and CMV disease. Conflicting: EBV viral load is currently negative — though EBV-negative PTLD occurs, especially late post-transplant.
2. **De novo B-cell malignancy unrelated to transplant immunosuppression** (e.g., chronic lymphocytic leukaemia/small lymphocytic lymphoma, splenic marginal zone lymphoma, or mantle cell lymphoma): Supported by age, splenomegaly, and monomorphic small-to-medium lymphoid cells. Conflicting: the acute systemic deterioration and inflammatory markers are less typical of indolent leukaemia.
3. **Rejection with secondary reactive lymphocytosis:** Less tenable now — a monoclonal population argues against a purely reactive process, and steroid response was minimal.

## 3. Timeline and Evidence Limits

The rejection episode was treated but graft function did not recover, suggesting an alternative cause of graft dysfunction. The records cannot yet establish: the lymphoma subtype, marrow involvement, disease stage, exact lymphocyte count/haemoglobin trend, LDH, or whether the intragraft infiltrate represents PTLD (the biopsy did not show diagnostic intravascular lymphoid infiltrate). Whether the borderline rejection is genuine or a reaction to an occult lymphoid infiltrate remains unresolved.
