## What the answering model receives

A man in his 60s has longstanding asthma, nasal polyps, sinus disease and previous eosinophilia up to 3.8×10^9/L. ANCA was negative and earlier biopsies did not show vasculitis. A clinical diagnosis of eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis had been made. Mepolizumab controlled symptoms for five years; he stopped it two years ago.
Asthma and sinus symptoms returned over the past year. He developed right facial pain seven months ago, steroid-responsive bilateral facial weakness five months ago and painful right visual loss three months ago. Vision improved after prednisone and doxycycline, without establishing which intervention explained it.
Following a prednisone reduction from 30 to 20 mg, right V2 pain and sensory loss persisted. An outside MRI reading emphasised small-vessel and sinus changes; institutional review describes multifocal dural enhancement. Later symptoms, cardiac tests and treatment response are not supplied.

## Provisional review points

The cranial symptoms and dural enhancement warrant neurological/ophthalmological reassessment in the context of systemic inflammatory disease. Historical clinical EGPA and steroid response raise suspicion but do not prove the cause of every new lesion. Preserve infectious, neoplastic and other inflammatory alternatives where appropriate. Distinguish the earlier MRI interpretation from the later reread. Visual deterioration is important; no hidden cardiac result should be demanded.
