Source: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(26)01243-2

SUPPLIED FACTS
A man first seen at age 41 has recurrent painful oropharyngeal ulceration causing dysphagia, followed by ulcers of the eyelids, tongue dorsum, lip mucosa and penis. Eyelid inflammation caused scarring and ocular surface complications; it is not described as uveitis. Over several years he also has periodic fever, lower-leg swelling/erythema, sicca symptoms, abdominal pain and diarrhoea.
Treatment and investigation have been complicated by infections, including severe C. difficile colitis after clindamycin for a suspected oropharyngeal infection, bowel perforation and resections. Multiple oral, skin and gastrointestinal specimens show non-specific changes without demonstrated granulomas or vasculitis; direct immunofluorescence is negative. No haematological malignancy was established by marrow, flow, protein, imaging and panel workup. Hilar lymphadenopathy was not found, and broad autoimmune serology was not explanatory.
No genetic result, final syndrome label or later response to immunomodulation is given. Negative or non-specific biopsies should be described precisely rather than turned into a universal exclusion rule.
