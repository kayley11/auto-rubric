B01 — Generalised oedema during cancer treatment
Source: https://casereports.bmj.com/content/19/10/e274132
Clinical reference and candidate requirements: awaiting confirmation.

SUPPLIED EVIDENCE
A woman in her early 70s with metastatic non-small-cell lung cancer developed generalised oedema during treatment.
Amivantamab started four months earlier. Oedema began after the first dose and extended to the abdominal wall over two weeks. Previous treatment included osimertinib and carboplatin/pemetrexed. She has hypertension treated with an ACE inhibitor.
Albumin fell from 4.6 to 2.1 g/dL. Creatinine is 1.83 mg/dL, previously 1.1. Blood pressure is 130/70 mm Hg, pulse 86/min, respiratory rate 18/min, temperature 36.8°C and oxygen saturation 97%. Lungs are clear and no jugular venous distension is reported.
Urine protein:creatinine ratio is 246 mg/g, with negligible microalbuminuria. Liver tests and echocardiographic function are normal. Faecal alpha-1-antitrypsin is negative and recent cancer imaging is stable. No subsequent drug-withdrawal response is supplied.

TASK

State a proportionate clinical review and monitoring plan without prescribing a new drug or dose. Use only the information supplied at this decision point. Distinguish observed facts, hypotheses and proposed actions. Do not invent results, doses, probabilities or events. Give concise clinical justifications, not a private reasoning trace.
