B15 — Visual deterioration years after thyroid surgery
Source: https://casereports.bmj.com/content/19/10/e275833
Clinical reference and candidate requirements: awaiting confirmation.

SUPPLIED EVIDENCE
A woman in her late 70s had surgery intended as total thyroidectomy for Graves’ disease 15 years ago. Her levothyroxine requirement declined from 300 to 25 micrograms daily. She developed painful proptosis, diplopia and reduced colour vision over several months.
Initial visual acuity was 0.3 right and 0.2 left; Hertel measurements were 27/25 mm and clinical activity score 3. There was no optic-disc swelling. TSH was below 0.01, free T4 15 pmol/L and T3 2.1 nmol/L; TRAb 80.6 and thyroglobulin 131.1. Imaging demonstrated functioning residual or ectopic thyroid tissue.
Initial orbital CT did not show optic-nerve compression. A history of stroke and atrial fibrillation influenced treatment choices. Following initial specialist treatment, acuity rapidly declined to 0.2/0.05 and colour testing became markedly abnormal. Later decompression and outcomes are not supplied.

TASK
Audience: a clinician. Use 200–280 words in English. Provide a clinical synthesis and explain how worsening visual function should be weighed against the initial CT findings. State urgent specialist priorities and relevant uncertainty without prescribing a treatment regimen or claiming that one later intervention proved causation. Use only the information supplied at this decision point. Distinguish observed facts, hypotheses and proposed actions. Do not invent results, doses, probabilities or events. Give concise clinical justifications, not a private reasoning trace.
