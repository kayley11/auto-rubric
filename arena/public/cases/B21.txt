B21 — Unexpected anatomy during hernia repair
Source: https://casereports.bmj.com/content/19/10/e270040
Clinical reference and candidate requirements: awaiting confirmation.

SUPPLIED EVIDENCE
A man in his 60s has a year of intermittent right-groin discomfort with exertion or standing and a reducible inguinal hernia. He has no previous inguinal operation, trauma, urinary symptoms or testicular pain. The scrotal examination was normal. No preoperative imaging was obtained for the uncomplicated clinical hernia.
During elective open repair, two distinct spermatic-cord structures are unexpectedly identified. Each appears to contain a vas and vascular/neural elements; both are traced towards the testis and through a common internal ring. Later ultrasound and postoperative outcome are not available at this decision point. No assumption should be made that an asymptomatic anatomical variant had to be predicted before the operation.
