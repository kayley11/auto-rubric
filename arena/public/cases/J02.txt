SUPPLIED FACTS
A 24-year-old man has depression, anxiety and opioid-use disorder, with two previous unintentional overdoses. Treatment includes buprenorphine, venlafaxine and prazosin. Mood and anxiety improved during follow-up, but severe daytime sleepiness persisted despite reducing venlafaxine. He falls asleep unintentionally on public transport and while reading, and reports sometimes sleeping for very prolonged periods.
Further history includes brief hand weakness when laughing, visual/tactile experiences while falling asleep and repeated awakening with inability to move. His partner has not reported snoring or apnoea. Several relatives have unspecified sleep disorders. He reports no recent non-prescribed opioid use but has recently used ketamine several times a week and alcohol weekly. Routine testing detects buprenorphine and its metabolite but not other tested opioids, benzodiazepines or stimulants; it does not establish absence of every substance.
BMI is 21 and neurological examination is unremarkable. Selected renal, electrolyte, glucose, liver and iron studies are not explanatory. Thyroid testing is not supplied. There is no polysomnography or multiple-sleep-latency result at this point.
