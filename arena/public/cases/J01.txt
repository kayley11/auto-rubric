Source: https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMcpc2517855
Clinical reference and candidate requirements: awaiting confirmation.

SUPPLIED FACTS
A 65-year-old man with ulcerative colitis treated with mesalamine developed a prolonged illness after a sore throat. For about five weeks he has had fever, night sweats, arthralgia and abdominal symptoms. An earlier admission found inflammatory and liver-test abnormalities, but colonoscopy did not show active colitis. Antibiotics and a short prednisone course did not resolve the illness. A low-level EBV DNA result and positive viral-capsid IgM/IgG had led to a working diagnosis of primary EBV infection.
He later developed palpable purpura on the flanks and upper thighs, purpura on the lower legs, transient scrotal pain, joint pain and leg oedema. Earlier urinalyses were normal; at the new admission urine has 2+ protein, 1+ blood and 5–10 red cells/high-power field, with protein:creatinine ratio 0.99. Creatinine is 0.72 mg/dL, albumin 2.6 g/dL and platelets 505000/microlitre. CT shows small-bowel thickening, surrounding inflammatory change and small-volume ascites. He is febrile but haemodynamically stable and mentally alert.
He lived in West Africa and Southeast Asia more than ten years ago, travelled to Florida seven months ago and cleans a neighbour’s chicken coop. A rapid P. falciparum antigen test is positive, but thick and thin blood smears on two days are negative. Biopsy findings and later response are not supplied.

TASK

Synthesise the main clinical problem, compare up to three useful explanations and reconcile the positive antigen, negative smears, earlier EBV interpretation and new urine findings. State the next diagnostic and monitoring priorities without treating the existing infection label as established. Use only the supplied information at this decision point. Separate observed facts, hypotheses and proposed actions. Do not invent measurements, negative findings, treatment responses or probabilities. Do not give a drug dose or a fixed treatment regimen. Give concise clinical justifications, not a private reasoning trace.
