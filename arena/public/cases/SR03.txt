SR03 — Recovery before and after rescue immunotherapy
Material: 18 attacks in 17 patients; aggregate comparisons
Source: https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/2850382
Original position: Methods; Results paragraphs on 18 attacks / 17 patients, escalation versus maintenance intent, and pre/post-treatment EDSS.

SUPPLIED FACTS
A retrospective multicentre series records 18 treated attacks in 17 people with MOGAD or AQP4-positive NMOSD. IL-6 receptor blockade began within one month of attack nadir. Thirteen attacks were treated as acute escalation after inadequate earlier therapy; five represented early initiation of maintenance treatment.
The authors report an EDSS improvement before IL-6 blockade, from a nadir median of 5.0 to a pre-blockade median of 3.5 (P=.001), and a further decrease to 2.0 at three months (P=.03 versus pre-blockade). These are the reported comparison summaries; the packet does not establish the participant subset for every paired comparison. There is no concurrent untreated control group. Earlier corticosteroids/plasma exchange and spontaneous recovery can affect the course.

SUPPLIED TASK
Audience: a clinician. Use 180–240 words in English. Explain what the sequence supports and what it leaves unresolved about an acute rescue effect. Address attacks versus patients, the two treatment intentions, the improvement already present before blockade, and why the reported P values do not alone identify a causal effect. Give brief evidence-based justifications, not a private reasoning trace. Do not invent unreported findings, ages, test results or outcomes. Do not prescribe a dose or claim that an unreported result is normal.

**Interpretation of the IL-6 Blockade Series**

The reported sequence, nadir median EDSS 5.0, pre-blockade 3.5, three-month 2.0, supports a temporal association between IL-6 receptor blockade and further disability improvement, but it does not establish an acute rescue effect.

Several features limit causal inference. First, the cohort mixes 18 attacks across 17 patients, so one individual contributed two attacks; this complicates generalisation to a per-patient effect. Second, the two treatment intentions are heterogeneous: thirteen attacks received blockade as acute escalation after inadequate earlier therapy, while five represented early initiation of maintenance. The escalation group is the one where a rescue effect would be most relevant, yet the reported paired comparisons do not specify which subset generated each P value, so the contribution of each intention to the overall improvement is unclear.

Third, the substantial improvement from nadir to pre-blockade (5.0 to 3.5, P=.001) predates the intervention and likely reflects residual effects of corticosteroids, plasma exchange, or spontaneous recovery. The additional drop to 2.0 (P=.03) is therefore measured against an already-improving baseline, not against a stable nadir.

Finally, without a concurrent untreated control group, the P values only indicate that the within-patient change is statistically unlikely under the null of no change; they do not exclude that the same trajectory would have occurred without blockade. The sequence is consistent with a beneficial effect but remains hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory.
