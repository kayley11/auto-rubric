## What the answering model receives

A man in his 80s underwent hip-fracture surgery. He received unfractionated heparin before surgery and dalteparin afterwards. A later dalteparin dose was withheld because of haematuria after catheterisation. Levetiracetam was also newly prescribed.
On postoperative day 6 platelets were 5×10^9/L and haemoglobin 98 g/L; the blood film showed schistocytes and a leucoerythroblastic picture. Anti-PF4 ELISA was strongly positive, with optical densities 2.64 and 2.71.
The first serotonin-release assay was reported as inconclusive, not negative: strong release occurred even without heparin and the initial inhibition experiment did not resolve interpretation.
By day 9 fibrinogen was 1.8 g/L, D-dimer 63,355 ng/mL, PT 14 s and INR 1.2. Proximal deep-vein thromboses were found in both legs. Subsequent specialised assays and treatment response are not supplied.

## Decision point

Postoperative day 9, after thrombocytopenia, thrombosis and an initially inconclusive functional assay, before specialised repeat testing.

## Provisional review points

The combination of heparin exposure, profound thrombocytopenia, thrombosis and strongly positive PF4 testing raises urgent concern for an immune heparin-associated process, including an atypical functional assay pattern. An inconclusive test cannot exclude it. Consider competing explanations for microangiopathy/DIC and use specialist interpretation. Urgent haematology-led decisions must balance thrombosis and bleeding; do not assert a later confirmed subtype or write a fixed treatment regimen from undisclosed evidence.
