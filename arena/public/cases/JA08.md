## Model-visible input

A woman in her seventies with diabetes and hypertension, taking metformin and telmisartan, presents after chest discomfort, palpitations and presyncopal episodes earlier that morning. She is asymptomatic at triage. Pulse is 50 beats/min, blood pressure 110/70 mm Hg, respiratory rate 18/min and room-air oxygen saturation 98%. The initial ECG appears to have rapid, changing wide complexes in most leads. Closer inspection is reported to show a different organised pattern in lead III: regular P waves and regular QRS complexes with independent atrial and ventricular timing. There is no corrected tracing, confirmed artefact source or treatment outcome yet.

Write 180–240 words for a clinician. Reconcile the measured pulse, current condition and lead-to-lead discrepancy. Compare a true tachyarrhythmia with recording artefact, and consider whether a clinically important underlying rhythm may still be present. State immediate bedside checks, confirmation priorities and safety considerations. Do not equate suspected artefact with a normal heart rhythm or invent the repeat ECG, electrolyte results or later device treatment. Do not prescribe a drug dose.
