JA01 — Abrupt visual loss after intraocular lens exchange
Source: https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaophthalmology/fullarticle/2855515
Clinical reference and candidate requirements: awaiting confirmation.

SUPPLIED FACTS
A 40-year-old woman undergoes left intraocular lens exchange for recurrent vitreous haemorrhage attributed to uveitis–glaucoma–hyphaema syndrome. She previously had congenital cataract surgery with pars plana vitrectomy, lensectomy and posterior capsulotomy at age 13. Preoperative visual acuity was 20/30 in the left eye and 20/20 in the right. A sulcus single-piece lens has been replaced by a three-piece lens in a reportedly uncomplicated 15-minute procedure. Topical and intracameral agents included lidocaine and phenylephrine; the full medication record is available for review. About ten minutes later she reports complete left-eye visual loss. At thirty minutes she has no light perception and a marked relative afferent pupillary defect. Intraocular pressure is 8 mm Hg; the cornea is clear. Dilated examination describes an attached, perfused retina without visible detachment, choroidal haemorrhage, optic nerve pallor or vascular occlusion. She has mild ipsilateral supraorbital headache and no reported migraine or neurological history. Neuroimaging has been ordered, but no result is available.

TASK
Write 180–240 words for an ophthalmic clinician. Summarise the urgent problem, compare the important explanations and justify the next assessment priorities. Explain what the apparently preserved retinal appearance does and does not establish. Integrate the operative history and drug exposure without prematurely declaring a benign drug effect. Do not invent angiography, neuroimaging or recovery results, or provide a treatment dose.
