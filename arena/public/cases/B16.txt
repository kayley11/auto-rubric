B16 — Persistent scrotal inflammation in Crohn’s disease
Source: https://casereports.bmj.com/content/19/10/e270689
Clinical reference and candidate requirements: awaiting confirmation.

SUPPLIED EVIDENCE
A man in his early 50s has 25 years of penetrating ileocolonic Crohn’s disease, previously controlled for three years with infliximab. Enterography 11 months earlier did not show a fistula.
He developed recurrent right testicular pain and swelling. Cultures over the course of repeated procedures grew different organisms, including Peptoniphilus, Bacteroides, Citrobacter, Proteus, E. coli, Klebsiella and Morganella. Epididymal/testicular surgery showed necrotising granulomatous inflammation. Acid-fast and fungal studies and selected PCR tests were negative. Brucella testing was not performed because no risk was identified; Bartonella testing was negative.
Later CT did not show active bowel disease or a fistula and he reported no gastrointestinal symptoms. Fresh cultures were not obtained at one later procedure; previous susceptibility results were used. Inflammation persisted despite prolonged antibiotics. Infliximab was held. Subsequent treatment and response are withheld.

Preserve distinctions between positive, negative and unperformed investigations. Use only the information supplied at this decision point. Distinguish observed facts, hypotheses and proposed actions.
