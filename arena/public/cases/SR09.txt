An adult with a biopsy-confirmed invasive periocular cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma has failed a trial of intralesional interleukin-2, with no subsequent treatment or outcome reported.

The team should review the full staging workup (depth, margin status, lymphovascular invasion, perineural invasion), confirm the histological grade, and reassess whether the tumour remains amenable to the same intralesional approach or whether surgical excision with clear margins is now indicated. The 81% complete-response figure from a single-centre uncontrolled series of 16 patients does not justify continuing unchanged treatment in this individual. That figure is a population-level observation from a small, uncontrolled, single-site cohort; it carries wide confidence intervals, is subject to selection and reporting bias, and cannot be extrapolated to a confirmed nonresponder. In this patient, the clinical failure of the trial is itself the most relevant data point: the tumour has demonstrated biological resistance to the agent under the conditions tested. Continuing the same intervention without modification ignores the individual's observed response and risks local progression, particularly in a periocular location where anatomical constraints may complicate later surgical salvage. The team should also review whether the initial intralesional technique (volume, concentration, number of injections) was adequate, whether the biopsy was representative of the full tumour burden, and whether any comorbidities or immunosuppressive factors might have attenuated the expected immune-mediated response. A multidisciplinary discussion incorporating ophthalmology, dermatologic surgery, and oncology is appropriate before selecting the next step.
