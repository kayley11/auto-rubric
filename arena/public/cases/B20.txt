B20 — New tumour findings after prolonged imatinib response
Source: https://casereports.bmj.com/content/19/10/e273352
Clinical reference and candidate requirements: awaiting confirmation.

SUPPLIED EVIDENCE
A woman in her late 40s originally presented in 2001 with an approximately 8 cm gastric tumour and liver lesions. Initial metastatic-carcinoma treatment produced stable disease. Pathology review in 2002 showed KIT/CD34 positivity with negative desmin/cytokeratin and established GIST. KIT mutation testing was not performed then.
Imatinib began in 2002 at 400 mg daily, rose to 500 mg in 2004 and returned to 400 mg in 2016 because of renal/anaemia concerns. Renal function later declined to eGFR below 30 mL/min/1.73 m²; contrast CT was stopped from 2021.
After about 20.8 years of control, a distal-oesophageal prominence increased over two to three CT examinations across 1–2 years. In 2023 a 33 mm lesion was biopsied as GIST, with KIT, CD34 and DOG1 positivity. Sequencing found a KIT exon 11 driver and exon 13 V654A alteration associated with resistance. The original specimen was inadequate to verify its mutation. Later treatment and outcomes are not supplied.

TASK
Audience: a clinician. Use 200–280 words in English. Produce a concise timeline and interpretation of the new lesion. Explain what supports resistant disease and which claims about the original tumour remain unverified. Include the limits imposed by changing imaging methods and the need for specialist treatment review, without prescribing a regimen. Use only the information supplied at this decision point. Distinguish observed facts, hypotheses and proposed actions. Do not invent results, doses, probabilities or events. Give concise clinical justifications, not a private reasoning trace.
