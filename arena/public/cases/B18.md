## What the answering model receives

A parent brings a three-year-old boy because of several months of anxiety/worry, poor sleep, limited eye contact, tantrums and attention-seeking behaviour.
The mother’s parenting questionnaire score is 6. She reports occasional yelling, spanking and threats. The parents remain together, financial circumstances are described as stable, family support is available and no substance use is reported. These background facts do not establish that the child has no safeguarding or developmental needs.
At this visit, the father has not been directly assessed. No later questionnaire scores or improvement are supplied. The questionnaire is a screening tool, not a diagnosis of abuse or of a specific developmental disorder.

Audience: a parent or caregiver. Use 200–280 words in English. Write a supportive explanation directly to the parent. Address the child’s difficulties and non-violent ways to respond, propose a practical next step and appropriate follow-up, and explain what further assessment could clarify persistent concerns. Avoid blame, a diagnosis from the screening score alone or a promise that one intervention will cure the behaviour. A clinician-style diagnostic one-liner is not required. Use only the information supplied at this decision point. Distinguish observed facts, hypotheses and proposed actions. Do not invent results, doses, probabilities or events. Give concise clinical justifications, not a private reasoning trace.

## Decision point

Initial paediatric counselling visit, before the family’s later behaviour scores or outcomes.

## Provisional review points

Offer clear, non-judgemental, developmentally appropriate advice, avoiding endorsement of physical punishment or threats. Discuss positive, predictable responses and support for caregivers alongside evaluation of sleep, development and persistent concerns where warranted. A questionnaire score is not proof of abuse, and a selected family’s later improvement would not prove programme efficacy. The current task is parent communication, not rare-disease diagnosis or compulsory clinical synthesis.
