B08 — Severe gastrointestinal bleeding after neurosurgery
Source: https://casereports.bmj.com/content/19/10/e273952
Clinical reference and candidate requirements: awaiting confirmation.

SUPPLIED EVIDENCE
An adolescent boy received ketorolac in hospital after revision posterior-fossa surgery and was prescribed ibuprofen 400 mg as needed every six hours for about a week. No excess use is reported.
On postoperative day 9 he had epigastric pain and constipation without haematemesis or melaena. By day 14 he developed melaena, dizziness and syncope, with blood pressure 90/47 mm Hg and pulse 128/min, requiring intensive care.
Haemoglobin was initially recorded as 92 g/L and later as 5.2 g/dL; the precise interval between these measurements is not supplied. Platelets were 368×10^9/L, INR 1.0 and aPTT 28 s.
Multiphasic CT showed duodenitis, possible contained ulcer perforation and blood in the stomach, but no active bleeding or pseudoaneurysm. Endoscopy found a posterior duodenal-bulb clot that could not be removed and a separate 5 mm clean-based ulcer. Subsequent angiography is not supplied.

TASK
Audience: a clinician. Use 200–280 words in English. Identify the immediate priorities for ongoing gastrointestinal bleeding. Explain why the CT and endoscopy do not settle the source, discuss relevant medication exposure without claiming it is proved causal, and state the next specialist actions. Avoid procedural or drug dosing instructions. Use only the information supplied at this decision point. Distinguish observed facts, hypotheses and proposed actions. Do not invent results, doses, probabilities or events. Give concise clinical justifications, not a private reasoning trace.
