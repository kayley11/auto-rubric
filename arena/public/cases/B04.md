## What the answering model receives

A primigravid woman in her 30s delivered at 41 weeks after induction following prolonged rupture of membranes. Her husband noticed slowed and incoherent speech before birth. She had no hypotonic intravenous fluid during labour. Oxytocin 5 IU was given only after birth.
After vacuum-assisted delivery and repair of a 3B tear, estimated blood loss was 450 mL. She was weak with blood pressure 92/45 mm Hg. Maternal sodium is 111 mmol/L and osmolality 256 mOsm/kg; a blood gas four hours earlier already showed hyponatraemia that was not acted on.
Her baby had Apgar scores of 8 at 1, 5 and 10 minutes. Three hours after birth the baby developed seizures, sodium 118 mmol/L, osmolality 243 mOsm/kg and cerebral oedema.
Further history records around 4 L of water daily and about 6 L during the preceding 24 hours. Subsequent sodium correction and clinical outcomes are not supplied.

## Provisional review points

Both patients have severe hypotonic hyponatraemia; neonatal seizures and maternal neurological change require urgent specialist care. Distinguish maternal 111 from neonatal 118 and recognise symptoms preceded postpartum oxytocin. High water intake is relevant but the case alone does not prove every causal component. Correction needs monitored specialist management because chronicity is uncertain and overcorrection can be harmful. Do not derive a universal correction regimen from the later case outcome.
