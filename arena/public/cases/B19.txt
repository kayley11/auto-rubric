B19 — Persistent caesarean-scar pain in preterm pregnancy
Source: https://casereports.bmj.com/content/19/10/e275318
Clinical reference and candidate requirements: awaiting confirmation.

SUPPLIED EVIDENCE
A woman in her late 20s, gravida 3 para 2 with two previous caesarean sections, is transferred at 28+4 weeks because of persistent left-sided scar pain and concern about a scar complication. She is stable without vaginal bleeding or ruptured membranes.
BMI is 35 and she has insulin-treated gestational diabetes, platelets 141×10^9/L and depression treated with sertraline. The fetal heart trace is reactive. Ultrasound estimates fetal weight at 2,500 g, above the 99th centile, with polyhydramnios (deepest vertical pocket 8.4 cm). Placenta is anterior without demonstrated abruption and umbilical-artery Doppler is normal.
Lower-segment thickness is reported as 2.7 mm on the right and 1.2 mm on the left, but imaging is technically limited. MRI shows scar thinning and is inconclusive for torsion, dehiscence or rupture. Later clinical progression, delivery timing and operative findings are not supplied.

Distinguish observed facts, hypotheses and proposed actions. Do not invent results, doses, probabilities or events. Give concise clinical justifications, not a private reasoning trace.
