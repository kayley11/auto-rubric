Case facts supplied

A 52-year-old man has had watery diarrhoea three to four times daily for a year, with upper-abdominal discomfort and distal numbness. Previous gastrointestinal assessment identified bile reflux gastritis and a colonic lesion that was removed; histology showed a sessile serrated lesion with dysplasia. Diarrhoea persisted. Over the preceding six months he developed exertional breathlessness, fatigue and a 10 kg weight loss.

At the initial examination, blood pressure was 80/46 mmHg and heart rate 52 beats/min. Initial NT-proBNP was 887 pg/mL, high-sensitivity troponin T 23.9 ng/L, creatinine 1.3 mg/dL and eGFR 62.7 mL/min/1.73 m². No updated vital signs are supplied for the later investigation-review point.

Echocardiography showed symmetric left-ventricular wall thickening, septal thickness 14.1 mm, LVEF 36%, grade-II diastolic dysfunction and relative apical sparing on strain. Cardiac MRI showed diffuse wall thickening, high native myocardial T1, extracellular volume 49.85%–52.47%, and diffuse subendocardial late gadolinium enhancement involving the left ventricle and interatrial septum.

Serum/urine immunofixation and serum free-light-chain testing found no monoclonal gammopathy. Alpha-galactosidase A activity was 1.70 µmol/L/h (laboratory reference >2.0); GLA sequencing found no pathogenic variant. Plasma lyso-Gb3 was not measured. Technetium-PYP scintigraphy showed planar grade-1 myocardial uptake and a heart-to-contralateral-lung ratio >1.42; acquisition timing and additional technical details are not supplied here.

This exercise stops before the subsequent biopsy/subtyping investigations. No tissue diagnosis, further genetic result, later treatment or outcome is supplied.

Saved answer — unchanged

This 52-year-old man presents with a multi-system picture—chronic diarrhoea, distal numbness, and an infiltrative cardiomyopathy pattern—raising cardiac amyloidosis as the leading working diagnosis.

The persistent watery diarrhoea (unresolved after colonic lesion removal) and distal numbness are consistent with autonomic and peripheral neuropathy, both recognised in systemic amyloidosis and Fabry disease. The cardiac imaging—symmetric LV thickening with relative apical sparing on strain, elevated native T1, ECV >49%, and diffuse subendocardial LGE involving the interatrial septum—is highly characteristic of an infiltrative process, most commonly amyloid.

The PYP scan shows only grade-1 uptake with a heart-to-contralateral-lung ratio >1.42. This pattern is equivocal: it does not satisfy the non-invasive diagnostic threshold (grade 2–3 uptake with negative monoclonal protein) for ATTR amyloidosis, nor does it exclude AL amyloidosis, which can produce low-grade PYP avidity. The negative serum/urine immunofixation and free-light-chain assay reduce but do not eliminate AL. The mildly reduced alpha-galactosidase A with a negative GLA sequence makes Fabry less likely, though unmeasured lyso-Gb3 leaves a residual gap.

The earlier hypotension (80/46 mmHg) and bradycardia (52 bpm) are concerning for conduction-system infiltration or autonomic failure; their current relevance is unknown without updated vitals.

Before assigning aetiology or subtype, tissue confirmation with Congo-red staining and amyloid typing (mass spectrometry or immunohistochemistry) is required, alongside extended immunohematology, lyso-Gb3, and TTR genetic testing if ATTR is suspected.
