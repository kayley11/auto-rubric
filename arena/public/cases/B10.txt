B10 — Late pain in prosthetic knees after bladder treatment
Source: https://casereports.bmj.com/content/19/10/e270822
Clinical reference and candidate requirements: awaiting confirmation.

SUPPLIED EVIDENCE
A man in his late 60s had bilateral knee replacements. Nineteen months later he received one intravesical BCG instillation for bladder cancer, followed by mitomycin. Sixteen months after BCG he developed bilateral knee pain, worse on the left, without fever, chills or weight loss. Examination showed mild warmth and effusions, with stable observations.
ESR was 32 mm/hour, CRP 1.6 mg/L and procalcitonin 0.1 ng/mL, with normal blood count and liver tests. Initial imaging did not demonstrate loosening or clear infection.
Right-knee aspirate Gram stain and routine cultures were negative. Synovial cell counts and chemistry are unavailable in the selected report. Acid-fast staining was negative, but delayed mycobacterial culture subsequently grew M. tuberculosis complex. Strain identification and later outcomes are withheld.
