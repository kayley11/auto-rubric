## Model-visible input

A 59-year-old man in coastal southern Norway developed tremor, headache, night sweats and persistent hiccups after a fall with a blow to the back of the head. He has type 2 diabetes and previous thymoma surgery. An initial MRI was reported as unremarkable. Routine blood tests were not explanatory; the initial working diagnosis was post-concussion syndrome.
Symptoms progressed over months to gait difficulty, falls, memory difficulty, dysphagia, resting tremor, bradykinesia and hypophonia. Dopamine-transporter imaging showed reduced uptake, prompting a Parkinson disease hypothesis, but levodopa did not improve symptoms. EEG showed mild encephalopathy, and CSF had 443 white cells/microlitre against a reference below 5, with protein 3.03 g/L.
