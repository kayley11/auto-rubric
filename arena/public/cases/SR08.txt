SUPPLIED FACTS
A prospective uncontrolled series enrolled 15 people with advanced cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma of the head and neck awaiting surgery. All received neoadjuvant capecitabine.

Two people with apparent complete clinical lesion resolution chose radiotherapy instead of surgery; another did not proceed to surgery after progression. Neoadjuvant capecitabine in advanced head-and-neck cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma: a 15-patient uncontrolled series

In this prospective uncontrolled series of 15 patients, clinical tumour reduction occurred in 10 of 15 (66.7%). Among the 12 who proceeded to surgery, 5 of 12 (41.7%) achieved a complete pathological response and 7 of 12 (58.3%) were downstaged.

These three endpoints measure distinct things. Clinical disappearance reflects radiological or physical assessment and may overestimate true tumour burden, as residual microscopic disease can persist. Pathological complete response is confirmed histologically after resection and is a stronger indicator of tumour eradication at the time of surgery. Durable cure, by contrast, requires sustained freedom from recurrence over a meaningful follow-up interval; this series had short follow-up, so durability cannot be assessed.

Because there was no comparator arm, no randomisation, and no control group, no claim of comparative efficacy can be made. The observed response rates are descriptive only. Regarding safety, the absence of grade 3 or higher treatment-related toxicity in 15 patients is reassuring but does not establish a safety profile; rare or delayed toxicities may not have been captured, and the small sample limits generalisability.

In summary, the data suggest biological activity of neoadjuvant capecitabine in this setting but do not support conclusions about superiority over standard care, long-term oncological benefit, or a definitive safety profile. Larger controlled trials with adequate follow-up are needed.
