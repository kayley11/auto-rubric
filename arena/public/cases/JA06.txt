## TIA vs Migraine: A Retinal Embolic Episode

A 30-year-old woman on oral contraceptives with migraine with visual aura experienced five minutes of painless left-eye visual loss affecting the inferior visual field. She noted this differed from her typical aura. Retinal photographs obtained during the episode showed a small intravascular retinal embolus with superior macular whitening that migrated distally and diminished on successive images. Symptoms resolved within minutes; post-event examination was neurologically normal.

Although the episode resolved spontaneously and she has a migraine history, neither feature excludes a vascular aetiology. Migraine aura typically involves bilateral or alternating visual phenomena, cortical spreading depression patterns, and is not confined to a single retinal vascular territory. The documented intravascular embolus with corresponding inferior field loss (superior retinal territory) is objective retinal evidence of a transient ischaemic event in the ophthalmic circulation. However, the embolic source remains unconfirmed; no imaging, cardiac, or shunt-study results are yet available to identify whether the source is carotid, cardiac, or paradoxical embolism.

Urgent further assessment should include brain and vascular imaging (MRI with diffusion-weighted sequences, carotid and vertebral artery imaging), cardiac evaluation (echocardiography, rhythm monitoring), and consideration of a bubble study or transcranial Doppler to exclude a right-to-left shunt. The oral contraceptive is a potential prothrombotic contributor but cannot be asserted as the sole cause without excluding structural sources.
