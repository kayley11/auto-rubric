B17 — New vascular symptoms across decades
Source: https://casereports.bmj.com/content/19/10/e271253

SUPPLIED EVIDENCE
One man has had three vascular presentations at different ages and sites.
In his 40s, tinnitus and temporal headache led to diagnosis of a left transverse/sigmoid dural arteriovenous fistula, type IIa+b with venous reflux. Three embolisations relieved symptoms and subsequent MRI remained clear for about 14 years.
In his 50s, pulsatile scalp symptoms, right-sided weakness/numbness and aphasia accompanied a new superior-sagittal-sinus fistula with multiple feeders. Three further embolisations achieved near-occlusion, with about five years of clear follow-up imaging.
In his 60s he developed right occipital pain, retromastoid tenderness and intermittent tinnitus. Angiography in 2018 showed a right transverse/sigmoid fistula classified as type I. The selected record does not specify a new neurological deficit. Later surveillance and symptom outcomes are not supplied. Do not require a treatment choice solely from the later outcome. Use only the information supplied at this decision point. Distinguish observed facts, hypotheses and proposed actions.
