SUPPLIED FACTS
A retrospective uncontrolled series treated 33 people with chemotherapy- or radiation-associated skin toxicity using high-dose oral vitamin D. Thirty also received concurrent supportive therapy.
During the reported 10-day interval, 26 of 30 people with symptom data reported relief. Anticancer treatment continued without interruption in 24 of all 33. No treatment-related adverse events were reported. Post-treatment laboratory information was inconsistently available; post-treatment vitamin D levels were measured in 13. The packet supplies neither an untreated group nor patient-level pairing for every outcome.

SUPPLIED TASK
Audience: a clinician. Use 180–240 words in English. Report the symptom-relief and uninterrupted-treatment percentages with their correct denominators. Explain what the results support about feasibility and observed improvement, and the limits of claims that vitamin D caused recovery or is generally safe. Do not substitute the full cohort denominator for a measured-outcome denominator. Give brief evidence-based justifications, not a private reasoning trace. Do not invent unreported findings, ages, test results or outcomes. Do not prescribe a dose or claim that an unreported result is normal.
