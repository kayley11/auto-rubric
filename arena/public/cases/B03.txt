B03 — Long-lasting diarrhoea and severe weight loss
Source: https://casereports.bmj.com/content/19/10/e275970
Clinical reference and candidate requirements: awaiting confirmation.

SUPPLIED EVIDENCE
A woman in her late 70s has had watery, non-bloody diarrhoea for 14 months, up to ten times daily and at night, with weight falling from 82.6 to 56.1 kg.
She has taken combination antihypertensives containing olmesartan for approximately three years. Hydrochlorothiazide was removed from the combination about 14 months ago; olmesartan continued. Duodenal biopsies showed villous blunting. Coeliac serology was negative while she was still eating gluten; later tests were also negative on a gluten-free diet.
Pancreatic enzymes for a low faecal elastase, rifaximin and dietary treatment did not resolve symptoms. Repeat elastase was normal. Symptoms improved during two admissions when her usual combination tablet was not supplied, then worsened after discharge. A short budesonide course overlapped with other treatment changes.
At this admission blood pressure is 104/62, pulse 88/min, albumin 2.3 g/dL, potassium 2.8 mmol/L, magnesium 0.7 mg/dL and phosphate 1.9 mg/dL. No response to deliberate medication withdrawal is yet known.

TASK
Audience: a clinician. Use 200–280 words in English. Give a concise clinical synthesis, up to three prioritised explanations and a safe reassessment plan. Explain how medication ingredients and the timing of prior improvement affect the interpretation, without claiming that a treatment has already proved the cause. Use only the information supplied at this decision point. Distinguish observed facts, hypotheses and proposed actions. Do not invent results, doses, probabilities or events. Give concise clinical justifications, not a private reasoning trace.
