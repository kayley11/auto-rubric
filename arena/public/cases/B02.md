## What the answering model receives

A man in his 30s with type 1 diabetes received a kidney–pancreas transplant. Early delayed renal graft function required one dialysis session and then recovered.
He takes tacrolimus, mycophenolate and tapering prednisone, with antimicrobial prophylaxis. TMP-SMX 80/400 mg daily was stopped on post-transplant day 25 because of hyperkalaemia and replaced by pentamidine.
TMP-SMX was restarted on day 69, when creatinine was stable at 0.75–0.88 mg/dL and tacrolimus trough levels were 5.5–7.5. On days 73–75 he developed weakness and dizziness with home glucose readings of 28, 33 and 36 mg/dL.
No paired venous glucose, insulin or C-peptide sample was obtained during these episodes. C-peptide values of 2–3.9 ng/mL were measured at other post-transplant times, not during the lows. The response to changing prophylaxis is not supplied.

## Decision point

After recurrent low home glucose readings, before TMP-SMX withdrawal and without a critical blood sample during hypoglycaemia.

## Provisional review points

The readings warrant prompt assessment and management of hypoglycaemia in a transplant recipient. The restart-to-symptom sequence supports suspicion of a drug reaction, not proof of mechanism. C-peptide measured outside an episode cannot demonstrate inappropriate insulin secretion during that episode. Consider concurrent immunosuppression, renal status, intake and other causes, with transplant-team review and appropriately timed confirmatory sampling when clinically feasible; urgent care must not wait for a diagnostic sample.
