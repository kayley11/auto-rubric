## What the answering model receives

A woman in her late 40s had two years of atraumatic left-hip pain, restricted movement and an iliopsoas mass. There was no relevant trauma, injection, prior surgery, anticoagulant exposure, neurological injury or bleeding history. Excision after unsuccessful conservative management showed mature heterotopic ossification.
On postoperative day 3 she became pale and oliguric, without fever, hypotension, recognised bleeding or sepsis. ## Decision point

Postoperative day 3 with new organ failure and haemolytic findings, before plasma exchange, dialysis response or recovery.

## Provisional review points

New severe renal impairment, thrombocytopenia and schistocytes require urgent nephrology/haematology assessment for thrombotic microangiopathy and alternatives. Supplied ADAMTS13 and coagulation tests inform the differential but should not be overextended. Low C3 may support further complement assessment without proving a genetic cause; missing genetic/antibody tests are unavailable, not negative. Preserve postoperative chronology and avoid causal certainty that surgery alone established a specific mechanism.
