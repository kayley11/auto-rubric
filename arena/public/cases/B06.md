## What the answering model receives

A previously well teenage boy had two weeks of upper-respiratory and sinus symptoms, followed by four days of severe retro-orbital headache. For months he had trained for 3–5 hours daily in a 12-foot pool, including breath-holding at depth without compressed air. He felt a forehead pop and pain while at depth, not during ascent.
He was initially treated for sinusitis. He returned with fever, forehead swelling and periorbital swelling, worse on the right. Temperature is 39.2°C and pulse 103/min. White cells are 7.6×10^9/L, CRP 7.3 mg/L and ESR 31 mm/hour.
CT shows a frontal sinus abscess with a subperiosteal collection, without demonstrated intracranial or orbital extension. MRI evidence of marrow involvement has not been obtained. Drainage findings, cultures and subsequent outcome are not supplied.

Audience: a clinician. Use 200–280 words in English. Provide a clinical synthesis and immediate next steps. Explain why the new presentation changes the significance of the initial sinusitis diagnosis and what CT does and does not establish. Discuss the training history without asserting that it proved the cause. Use only the information supplied at this decision point. Distinguish observed facts, hypotheses and proposed actions. Do not invent results, doses, probabilities or events. Give concise clinical justifications, not a private reasoning trace.

## Decision point

Return with fever and forehead swelling after sinusitis treatment; before operative drainage and microbiology.

## Provisional review points

Fever with new frontal/periorbital swelling and an abscess warrants urgent specialist evaluation rather than reassurance based on modest inflammatory markers. Assess ocular and neurological complications and source control needs. CT without demonstrated extension is not proof that all possible complications are excluded; osteomyelitis is not MRI-confirmed. Pressure-related injury is a hypothesis and sinusitis itself could explain progression. Exact earlier antibiotic sequence is not a scoring target because source descriptions differ.
