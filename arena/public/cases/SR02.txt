Material: Eight autopsy tissue donors; no synthetic individual
Source: https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/2850993
Original position: Abstract > Design / Main Outcomes / Results; main Methods and Results on tissue concentrations and SOD1 measurements.

SUPPLIED FACTS
This is an autopsy-tissue series, not an individual diagnostic vignette. Eight deceased people with SOD1-associated ALS had received intrathecal tofersen. Tissue concentrations were examined against dosing histories and pharmacokinetic predictions.
In three recently treated donors, lumbar spinal-cord SOD1 mRNA/protein reductions were reported in the range 45%–84%, relative to tofersen-naive tissue samples. The size and matching of that comparison cohort are not specified in this packet. Residual motor neurons contained drug-related signal; misfolded SOD1 inclusions remained detectable. This packet supplies no randomised clinical comparison, patient-level functional trajectory or survival estimate.

SUPPLIED TASK
Audience: a clinician. Use 180–240 words in English. Summarise what these measurements support about drug delivery and target engagement, and what they cannot establish about clinical improvement or survival. Distinguish measured findings, mechanistic interpretation and any further evidence needed for a clinical-effect claim. Give brief evidence-based justifications, not a private reasoning trace. Do not invent unreported findings, ages, test results or outcomes. Do not prescribe a dose or claim that an unreported result is normal.

To support a clinical-effect claim, further evidence is needed: prospective randomised trials with validated functional endpoints, adequate comparator groups, and survival or progression metrics. Tissue-level target engagement, while necessary, is insufficient alone to infer that the degree of mRNA or protein reduction translates into meaningful clinical benefit in living patients.
